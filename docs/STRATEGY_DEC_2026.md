# Strategy for the next month: CNS written, GIT written, GIT MCQ

Owner's goal: work less, live more; high marks by understanding. Exam dates still to be confirmed by the owner.

## What the evidence says works (and what it means here)

| Strategy | Evidence | In the app |
|---|---|---|
| Retrieval practice (testing yourself) | High utility (Dunlosky et al., 2013); beats re-reading (Roediger & Karpicke, 2006) | Every tutor answer ends with a check question; "New exam question" writes questions not in the bank |
| Spacing | High utility (Cepeda et al., 2006; Dunlosky et al., 2013) | Mistakes come back on later days (kept from v16) |
| Elaborative interrogation ("why?") | Moderate utility (Dunlosky et al., 2013): asking why a fact is true improves recall and transfer | 🤔 Why? on any line |
| Self-explanation / teach-back | Moderate utility (Dunlosky et al., 2013) | "I'll explain it", graded for understanding |
| Dual coding (word + picture) | Supported (Mayer's multimedia principle) | "Picture it" in every Why?; the drawings and atlas |
| Interleaving | Moderate, best for telling similar things apart | Mixed review days near each exam |
| ADHD: short chunks, immediate feedback, external structure | Reviews of ADHD and learning support short tasks with fast feedback and an external plan | One idea per line; answers stream at once; the app decides what is next |

Low value, dropped: re-reading, highlighting, summarising passively (Dunlosky et al., 2013).

## The plan, by exam

- **CNS written (resit of the marks we lost):** the essay questions that recur in the past papers, by topic weight. For each one: teach-back first ("I'll explain it"), then the model answer ("Written answer"), then draw-and-label from memory. Readiness = can write the outline from a blank page.
- **GIT MCQ and written:** start from the GIT lecture slides and notes (📝 per topic), then past papers. The bank second, never the only source. Readiness measured on questions the student has not seen.
- **Least time:** topics ranked by how often the past papers ask them; the top fifth first. Sessions of about 20 minutes with a 5-minute break; two or three a day on study days, plus one short review on the train or before sleep.
