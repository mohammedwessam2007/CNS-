# ADHD and learning: what the evidence says, and what the app does about it (v17)

The owner asked (24 Sep 2026): *"I have ADHD so gamify it, but don't amputate or ruin it. Research what works best for someone with ADHD and build it in."* This file lists the findings, how strong each one is, and the feature built from it. Nothing that already worked was removed. The schedule, the MCQ engine, the notes, the mocks and the daily time budget are unchanged. The new layer only adds feedback, structure you can see, and pictures you can touch.

Evidence grades: **A** = meta-analysis or several RCTs. **B** = controlled studies or a strong, consistent theory. **C** = expert consensus or design practice.

## Findings → design

| # | Finding | Grade | What the app does |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Delay aversion and steep delay discounting.** In meta-analyses, people with ADHD choose small immediate rewards over larger delayed ones more often than controls, and real rewards roughly double that effect ([Marx et al. 2021](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1087054718772138); [Yu & Sonuga-Barke 2020](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1087054716661231); [Transl. Psychiatry 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11971471/)). | A | Every answer pays at once: XP appears on the verdict, a combo counter climbs, and the progress ring for the sprint fills in the same frame. The exam (the big, delayed reward) is broken into rewards measured in seconds. |
| 2 | **Continuous beats partial reinforcement.** With reward after every response, children with ADHD performed like controls. With partial reward they fell behind and got frustrated ([Douglas & Parry](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF02168075); [review, Luman et al. 2005](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0272735804001527); [Hulsbosch et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1177/10870547261430072)). | B | **Every** answer earns XP, including a wrong one (+4: *you learned the trap*). No random loot boxes and no variable-ratio rewards. Nothing is ever taken away: no response cost, no lost XP, no "streak lost" screen. |
| 3 | **Immediate feedback.** People with ADHD choose tasks with immediate feedback more often, and feedback timing changes how well they learn ([Sci. Rep. 2018](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-018-33551-3)). | B | Already in v16: every answer is explained right away. v17 adds the matching live diagram under the explanation, set to the exact fact the question tested. |
| 4 | **Externalize time, rules and motivation at the point of performance.** Barkley: ADHD is a disorder of self-regulation over time. Help works when it is physical and visible *where the work happens* ([Barkley fact sheet](https://www.russellbarkley.org/factsheets/ADHD_EF_and_SR.pdf)). Time perception is consistently impaired in ADHD, and time-assistive devices improved time management in an RCT ([ADHD Evidence summary](https://www.adhdevidence.org/blog/time-blindness-found-to-be-a-consistent-feature-of-adhd)). | A (time deficit) / B (devices) | A **sprint bar** always on screen: the sprint's dots (●●●○○○○○), minutes left today, level progress, and today's quests. A soft **sprint timer ring** shows elapsed time as a shape, not a number. |
| 5 | **Working memory is limited; chunking helps.** Working-memory deficits are central in ADHD. Chunking cuts load ([Neuroimage Clin. 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2213158221001066); [eLife 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11870651/)). | A | Work comes in **sprints of 8** with a clear end. Each diagram shows one idea at a time: one tract lit, one lesion, one artery territory. Labels appear when you tap, not all at once. |
| 6 | **Short bursts of exercise.** A single bout of moderate-to-vigorous activity improves inhibitory control in ADHD; about 5 minutes of jumping was enough in some studies ([PLOS One 2023 meta-analysis](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0289732); [Sci. Rep. 2018](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-018-30067-8); [adults, 2019](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6443849/)). Effects on working memory are mixed. | A (inhibition) / C (memory) | After each sprint: an optional **2-minute move break** with a visual countdown (jumping jacks, stairs, water). It is always optional, so hyperfocus is never broken by force. The existing v13 fatigue reset stays. |
| 7 | **Retrieval practice works just as well with ADHD,** but it is hard to keep doing it consistently ([college students with ADHD, Learning & Instruction](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0959475219305092); [2023 follow-up](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10397724/)). | A | Kept: the v15 "pull it from memory" steps and the v16 spaced MCQs. New: **"Spot it" drills** on the diagrams (tap the lateral spinothalamic tract, 5 in a row, instant ✓/✗). Consistency comes from the quests and the forgiving streak, not from willpower. |
| 8 | **Gamification helps learning.** Meta-analysis: g = 0.49 on cognitive outcomes (stable in rigorous studies), 0.36 on motivation, 0.25 on behaviour. Game fiction and a mix of competition and collaboration were the strongest moderators ([Sailer & Homner 2020](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1245270)). | A | Light game fiction on top of the real work: ranks named after the nervous system (Neuron → Synapse → Reflex arc → Tract → Nucleus → Cortex → Connectome), mocks are **boss fights**, and a mistake that returns is a **rematch**. There is no competition, because it is a single learner. |
| 9 | **Game-based treatment can move attention.** The EndeavorRx RCT (n = 348): attention (TOVA API) improved 0.93 vs 0.03 for the control, about 25 min/day, 5 days a week ([STARS-ADHD](https://www.akiliinteractive.com/news-collection/2023/1/6/pivotal-trial-of-endeavorrx-in-adolescents-with-adhd-shows-robust-improvements-in-attention-and-broader-clinical-outcomes); [serious-games review 2025](https://games.jmir.org/2025/1/e60937)). | A (for that product) | We do **not** claim a treatment effect. We take the design lessons: short daily doses, adaptive challenge, constant feedback. |
| 10 | **Novelty and interest capture attention** in ADHD. Predictable, distant, abstract rewards compete badly ([overview](https://www.additudemag.com/brain-stimulation-and-adhd-cravings-dependency-and-regulation/)). | C | Every lesson section with a matching diagram opens with a **live, touchable diagram**: lesion simulators, a field-defect simulator, pathway animations and a CSF flow you can block. A different kind of interaction on different days. |
| 11 | **Streaks work through loss aversion and can cause anxiety.** A streak freeze reduced drop-off in at-risk users by about 21% ([analysis](https://dev.to/pocket_linguist/why-duolingos-gamification-works-and-when-it-doesnt-1d4); [design review](https://uxmag.com/articles/the-psychology-of-hot-streak-game-design-how-to-keep-players-coming-back-every-day-without-shame)). | C | A **forgiving streak**: it counts study days, starts with 2 freezes, earns 1 more per 7-day run (at most 3), and a missed day uses one quietly. If none are left, the app says "welcome back" and starts again with no shame screen. |
| 12 | **Animations beat static pictures when they show real change** (d = 0.37 overall; 0.40 when representational; about 1.06 for procedures) ([Höffler & Leutner 2007](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0959475207001077)). | A | Animation is used only where something moves: an impulse running up a tract, CSF flowing, the basilar membrane peaking at a frequency, endolymph lagging in a canal. Decoration is kept out. `prefers-reduced-motion` turns animation off. |
| 13 | **Token economies** (points for behaviour) have large classroom effects: weighted ES ≈ 0.82 ([School Psychology Review 2016](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.17105/SPR45-4.379-399)). Behavioural treatment for ADHD, ES ≈ 0.74. | A | XP is the token. It is earned by *study behaviour that predicts marks*: finishing a lesson, answering, fixing a mistake, finishing a mock. It is never earned for idling or tapping around. Quests tie bonus XP to the three things that move the grade each day. |

## What was deliberately not done

- **No penalties** (finding 2, and frustration sensitivity): no XP loss, no hearts, no lives, no "streak lost".
- **No leaderboards or social pressure.** There is one learner.
- **No forced breaks** that cut hyperfocus. Breaks are offered, never imposed. v13's fatigue reset, which responds to a run of misses, is unchanged.
- **No extra minutes.** The 2-hour daily budget and its tests are unchanged. The game layer adds no questions and no steps; drills and breaks are optional.
- **No sounds by default.** Haptics and sound are opt-in (quiet library or class). Motion follows the device's reduce-motion setting.
- **Nothing hidden in the sealed mocks.** The bar shows the timer and progress only. No diagrams or hints appear before submission.

## The pictures: why hand-built diagrams

The earlier pictures were the best free image found for a term (Wikipedia/Commons). They are often generic ("spinal cord, labeled diagram") and cannot show *this* fact. v17 adds a hand-drawn **CNS atlas** of interactive SVG diagrams. Each is drawn to standard anatomy, labelled in the notes' own words, and set to the exact state a question tests (e.g. the right half of the cord cut at C2, the left optic tract, the aqueduct blocked). The live diagram opens its section; the department's own drawings (v16.2) and the photos stay right below it as extra views. Details: `docs/V17_RECEIPT.md`.
