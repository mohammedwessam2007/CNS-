## VII

But all these are golden dreams. Oh, tell me, who was it who first announced, who first proclaimed, that man does nasty things only because he does not know his real interests; and that if he were enlightened, if his eyes were opened to his real, normal interests, he would at once stop doing nasty things and at once become good and noble, because, being enlightened and understanding his real advantage, he would see his own advantage precisely in the good; and it is well known that no man can knowingly act against his own advantage; so he would, so to speak, start doing good out of necessity? Oh, the babe! Oh, the pure, innocent child! Why, first of all, when has it ever happened, in all these thousands of years, that man acted only out of his own advantage? What is to be done with the millions of facts that show how people knowingly, that is, fully understanding their real advantage, have pushed it into second place and rushed off down another road, into risk, into chance, with nobody and nothing forcing them to, but as if simply not wanting the road that had been pointed out to them; and how they stubbornly, wilfully forced their way along another road, a hard, absurd one, searching for it almost in the dark? So this stubbornness and wilfulness must really have been more pleasant to them than any advantage...

Advantage! What is advantage? And will you take it upon yourself to define exactly what human advantage consists of? What if it turns out that human advantage sometimes not only can but even must consist precisely in wishing, in some cases, for what is bad for you and not what is advantageous? And if that is so, if such a case is even possible, then the whole rule goes to dust. What do you think, does such a case ever happen? You are laughing; laugh, gentlemen, but answer me this: have human advantages been counted up absolutely correctly? Aren’t there some that not only have not fitted, but cannot fit, into any classification? After all, gentlemen, as far as I know, you took your whole register of human advantages as averages from statistical figures and the formulas of economic science. Your advantages are prosperity, wealth, freedom, peace and quiet, and so on and so on; so that a man who, for example, openly and knowingly went against this whole register would be, in your opinion, and of course in mine too, an obscurantist or completely mad, wouldn’t he?

But here is what is astonishing: how does it happen that all these statisticians, sages and lovers of the human race, when they count up human advantages, keep leaving out one advantage? They don’t even take it into account in the form in which it ought to be taken, and the whole calculation depends on that. It wouldn’t be much of a disaster: just take this advantage and add it to the list. But the trouble is precisely that this tricky advantage does not fall into any classification and does not fit on any list. I have a friend, for example... Oh, gentlemen! He is your friend too; and whose friend, whose, isn’t he? When he is getting ready to do something, this gentleman will at once set out for you, eloquently and clearly, exactly how he must act according to the laws of reason and truth. More than that: he will talk to you with excitement and passion about real, normal human interests; he will mockingly reproach the short-sighted fools who understand neither their own advantage nor the real meaning of virtue; and then, exactly a quarter of an hour later, without any sudden outside cause, but precisely because of something inside him that is stronger than all his interests, he will do something completely different; that is, he will plainly go against everything he himself was saying: against the laws of reason, against his own advantage, well, in a word, against everything... I should warn you that my friend is a collective figure, and so it is somehow hard to blame him alone. That’s just it, gentlemen: doesn’t there really exist something that is dearer to almost every man than his very best advantages? Or (so as not to break the rules of logic) isn’t there one most advantageous advantage (the very one that gets left out, the one we were just talking about), which is more important and more advantageous than all the others, and for the sake of which a man, if need be, is ready to go against all laws, that is, against reason, honour, peace, prosperity; in a word, against all these beautiful and useful things, just to reach this first and most advantageous advantage, which is dearer to him than anything?

“Well, it’s still an advantage all the same,” you interrupt me. Allow me, sir, we will get to the bottom of this; and the point is not a play on words, but that this advantage is remarkable precisely because it destroys all our classifications and constantly smashes all the systems that lovers of the human race have built for the happiness of the human race. In a word, it gets in the way of everything. But before I name this advantage for you, I want to compromise myself personally, and so I boldly declare that all these beautiful systems, all these theories of explaining to mankind its real, normal interests, so that, striving inevitably to achieve them, it would at once become good and noble, are for the time being, in my opinion, nothing but logic-chopping! Yes, sir, logic-chopping! For to assert this theory of renewing the whole human race through the system of its own advantage is, in my opinion, almost the same as... well, as asserting, for example, after Buckle, that civilisation makes man gentler, and therefore less bloodthirsty and less capable of war. By logic, it seems, that is how it comes out for him. But man is so attached to systems and abstract conclusions that he is ready to distort the truth on purpose, ready to see nothing and hear nothing, just to justify his logic. I take this example because it is such a glaring one. Just look around you: blood flows in rivers, and so merrily, as if it were champagne. There you have our whole nineteenth century, the century Buckle lived in too. There you have Napoleon, both the great one and the present one. There you have North America, the everlasting union. There you have, finally, the caricature of Schleswig-Holstein... And what is it that civilisation softens in us?

Civilisation only develops in man a greater variety of sensations and... absolutely nothing more. And through developing this variety, man may perhaps reach the point of finding pleasure in blood. That has already happened to him, after all. Have you noticed that the most refined shedders of blood have almost always been the most civilised gentlemen, to whom all those Attilas and Stenka Razins sometimes could not hold a candle? And if they don’t catch the eye as vividly as Attila and Stenka Razin, it is precisely because they are met with too often, are too ordinary, too familiar. At any rate, civilisation has made man, if not more bloodthirsty, then certainly more nastily and vilely bloodthirsty than before. Before, he saw justice in bloodshed and exterminated whoever he thought deserved it with a calm conscience; while now, although we consider bloodshed a vile thing, we still go in for this vile thing, and even more than before. Which is worse? Decide for yourselves. They say Cleopatra (forgive the example from Roman history) liked to stick gold pins into the breasts of her slave girls and took pleasure in their screams and writhing. You will say that this was in times that were, relatively speaking, barbaric; that times are barbaric now too, because (also relatively speaking) pins are still being stuck in now; that even now, although man has sometimes learned to see more clearly than in barbaric times, he is still far from having learned to act as reason and science direct him. But all the same you are perfectly sure that he will certainly learn, once certain old bad habits have quite passed away, and once common sense and science have completely re-educated human nature and pointed it in the normal direction. You are sure that then man himself will stop making mistakes of his own free will and will, so to speak, be unable, even if he wanted to, to set his will against his normal interests.

More than that: then, you say, science itself will teach man (though this is a luxury already, in my opinion) that in reality he has neither will nor caprice, and never has had, and that he himself is nothing more than something like a piano key or the stop of an organ; and that, besides, there are also the laws of nature in the world; so that everything he does is done not at all by his own wishing but by itself, according to the laws of nature. Consequently, these laws of nature only have to be discovered, and man will no longer be answerable for his actions, and life will be extremely easy for him. All human actions will then, of course, be calculated according to these laws, mathematically, like a table of logarithms up to 108,000, and entered in a calendar; or better still, certain well-meaning publications will appear, like our present encyclopaedic dictionaries, in which everything will be so precisely calculated and labelled that there will no longer be any actions or adventures in the world.

Then, and all this is what you are saying, new economic relations will arrive, all ready-made and also calculated with mathematical precision, so that in an instant every possible question will vanish, simply because every possible answer will have been supplied. Then the crystal palace will be built. Then... well, in a word, then the golden age will come flying down. Of course, there is no way to guarantee (this is me speaking now) that it won’t then be, for example, terribly boring (for what is there to do when everything has been calculated in a table?), but on the other hand everything will be extremely sensible. Of course, what won’t you think up out of boredom! Gold pins are stuck in out of boredom too, after all; but none of that would matter. What is bad (this is me speaking again) is that, for all I know, people might even be glad of gold pins then. For man is stupid, phenomenally stupid. That is, he is not stupid at all, but he is so ungrateful that you could search and not find another like him. I, for example, would not be in the least surprised if suddenly, for no reason at all, in the middle of all that future universal good sense, some gentleman should appear with an ignoble, or rather a reactionary and mocking, face, put his hands on his hips and say to us all: “Well, gentlemen, why don’t we kick all this good sense to dust, once and for all, with one kick, for the sole purpose of sending all these logarithms to the devil, so that we can live again according to our own stupid will!” That would be nothing, but what is galling is that he would certainly find followers: that is how man is made. And all this from the emptiest of reasons, which hardly seems worth mentioning: namely, that man, always and everywhere, whoever he may be, has liked to act as he wanted, and not at all as reason and advantage commanded him; and one can want against one’s own advantage, and sometimes one positively must (that is my idea). One’s own free and unfettered wanting, one’s own caprice, however wild, one’s own fancy, provoked sometimes even to the point of madness: that is the very advantage that gets left out, the most advantageous advantage, which fits no classification and which constantly sends all systems and theories flying to the devil. And where did all these sages get the idea that man needs some normal, some virtuous wanting? Why did they imagine that man must have a sensibly advantageous wanting? What man needs is only independent wanting, whatever that independence may cost and wherever it may lead. And as for wanting, the devil only knows...
