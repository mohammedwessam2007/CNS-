## V

Well, can a man respect himself at all, can he possibly, when he has tried to find pleasure even in the very feeling of his own humiliation? I am not saying this now out of some sugary repentance. And in general I could never stand saying, “Sorry, Papa, I won’t do it again”: not because I was incapable of saying it, but on the contrary, perhaps precisely because I was only too capable of it, and how! As if on purpose, I would land in it just when I was not to blame, not even in my dreams. That was the vilest thing of all. And then once again I would be moved to the depths of my soul, repent, shed tears and, of course, deceive myself, although I was not pretending at all. My heart somehow played dirty there... Here I could not even blame the laws of nature, although the laws of nature have constantly offended me, more than anything, all my life. It is vile to remember all this, and it was vile then too. For a minute later, it used to be, I would realise spitefully that it was all a lie, a lie, a disgusting put-on lie: all those repentances, all those tender feelings, all those vows to be reborn. And if you ask why I twisted and tormented myself like that, the answer is: because it was so very boring to sit with folded arms, so I went in for these contortions. Really, that is how it was. Watch yourselves more closely, gentlemen, and you will understand that it is so. I invented adventures for myself and made up a life, just so as to live somehow.

How many times it has happened to me, well, for example, to take offence, just like that, for no reason, on purpose; and you know yourself, of course, that you took offence for no reason, that you put it on, but you work yourself up to such a point that in the end, really, you are genuinely offended. All my life I have been drawn to pulling stunts like this, so that in the end I was no longer master of myself. Another time I wanted to fall in love by force, twice in fact. And I suffered, gentlemen, I assure you. Deep down you don’t believe that you are suffering, mockery stirs, and yet I suffer, and in a real, genuine way at that; I am jealous, I am beside myself... And all from boredom, gentlemen, all from boredom; inertia crushed me. For the direct, legitimate, immediate fruit of consciousness is inertia, that is, conscious sitting-with-folded-arms. I mentioned this earlier. I repeat, I repeat emphatically: all spontaneous people and men of action are active precisely because they are dull and limited. How to explain this? Like this: because of their limitations they take the nearest, secondary causes for first causes, and so they persuade themselves more quickly and easily than others that they have found an unshakeable foundation for what they do, and they calm down; and that is the main thing. For in order to begin to act, you must first be completely at peace, with no doubts left at all. Well, and how am I, for example, to set myself at peace? Where are my first causes for me to rest on, where are my foundations? Where will I get them from? I exercise myself in thinking, and so with me every first cause at once drags another after it that is still more first, and so on to infinity. That is precisely the essence of all consciousness and thinking. So here again it is the laws of nature. And what is the result in the end? Why, the same thing. Remember: just now I was talking about revenge. (You probably didn’t take it in.) It is said: a man takes revenge because he finds justice in it. That means he has found a first cause, he has found a foundation, namely justice. So he is at peace on all sides, and therefore he takes his revenge calmly and successfully, being convinced that he is doing an honest and just deed. But I see no justice in it, and I find no virtue in it either, and so if I do start taking revenge, it will only be out of spite. Spite, of course, could overcome everything, all my doubts, and so could serve perfectly well in place of a first cause, precisely because it is not a cause. But what am I to do if I don’t even have spite (that is where I started just now, after all)? My anger, once again because of those accursed laws of consciousness, undergoes chemical decomposition. You look, and the object evaporates, the reasons vanish into thin air, the guilty party cannot be found, the insult becomes not an insult but fate, something like toothache, for which nobody is to blame; and so once again only the same way out is left: to beat the wall harder. So you give it up with a wave of the hand, because you have not found a first cause. But try letting yourself be carried away by your feeling blindly, without reasoning, without a first cause, driving consciousness away at least for a while; hate or love, just so as not to sit with folded arms. By the day after tomorrow at the very latest you will begin to despise yourself for having knowingly fooled yourself. Result: a soap bubble, and inertia. Oh, gentlemen, perhaps the only reason I consider myself an intelligent man is that all my life I have not been able either to begin or to finish anything. All right, all right, I am a chatterbox, a harmless, irritating chatterbox, like all of us. But what is to be done if the direct and only calling of every intelligent man is chatter, that is, deliberately pouring water from one empty jug into another?

## VI

Oh, if only I did nothing simply out of laziness! Lord, how I would respect myself then. I would respect myself precisely because I would at least be capable of laziness; there would be at least one quality in me that looked positive, one I could be sure of. Question: who is he? Answer: a lazybones. Why, it would be most pleasant to hear that about oneself. It would mean I was positively defined; it would mean there was something to say about me. “A lazybones!” Why, that is a title and a calling; it is a career, sir. Don’t laugh, it is so. I would then be a member of the very best club by right, and my only occupation would be respecting myself without a break. I knew a gentleman who was proud all his life of being a connoisseur of Lafite. He considered it his positive merit and never doubted himself. He died not merely with a calm conscience but with a triumphant one, and he was entirely right. And I would have chosen a career for myself then: I would have been a lazybones and a glutton, but not a simple one; one, say, who sympathised with everything beautiful and sublime. How do you like that? I have dreamed of it for a long time. This “beautiful and sublime” has pressed hard on the back of my neck in my forty years; but that is in my forty years, while then, oh, then it would have been different! I would at once have found myself a suitable activity, namely: drinking to the health of everything beautiful and sublime. I would seize on every occasion first to shed a tear into my glass and then to drink it to everything beautiful and sublime. I would turn everything in the world into the beautiful and sublime; in the vilest, most unarguable trash I would find the beautiful and sublime. I would become as tearful as a wet sponge. An artist, for example, paints a picture in the fashionable manner of Ge. At once I drink to the health of the artist who painted it, because I love everything beautiful and sublime. An author writes a piece called *As Anyone Pleases*; at once I drink to the health of “anyone”, because I love everything “beautiful and sublime”. I will demand respect for myself for this; I will hound anyone who does not show me respect. I live in peace, I die in triumph: why, that is charming, perfectly charming! And I would grow myself such a belly, build myself such a triple chin, develop such a ruby nose, that everyone I met would say, looking at me: “Now that’s a plus! That’s something really positive!” And say what you like, gentlemen, it is very pleasant to hear remarks like that in our negative age.
