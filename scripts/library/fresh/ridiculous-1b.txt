## IV

You see, again: very well, let it have been only a dream! But the feeling of the love of those innocent and beautiful people has remained in me forever, and I feel that their love pours down on me from there even now. I saw them myself; I came to know them and was convinced; I loved them, I suffered for them afterwards. Oh, I understood at once, even then, that in much I would not understand them at all. To me, as a modern Russian progressive and a vile Petersburger, it seemed insoluble, for instance, that they knew so much and yet had no science like ours. But I soon understood that their knowledge was filled and fed by other insights than ours on earth, and that their aspirations were quite different too. They desired nothing and were at peace; they did not strive to know life as we strive to understand it, because their life was full. But their knowledge was deeper and higher than our science; for our science seeks to explain what life is, it strives to understand it in order to teach others how to live; while they knew how to live without science. That I understood, but I could not understand their knowledge.

They would point out their trees to me, and I could not understand the degree of love with which they looked at them: as if they were talking with creatures like themselves. And you know, perhaps I won’t be wrong if I say that they did talk with them! Yes, they had found their language, and I am convinced that the trees understood them. That was how they looked at all of nature: at the animals, which lived with them peacefully, did not attack them, and loved them, conquered by their love. They pointed out the stars to me and spoke with me about them, about something I could not understand; but I am convinced that they were somehow in contact with the stars of heaven, not only in thought but in some living way.

Oh, these people did not even try to make me understand them; they loved me without that. But I knew that they would never understand me either, and so I hardly told them anything about our earth. I only kissed, in front of them, the earth they lived on, and worshipped them themselves without words; and they saw it and let themselves be worshipped, though they were ashamed that I worshipped them, because they themselves loved so much. They did not suffer for me when, in tears, I sometimes kissed their feet, knowing joyfully in my heart with what power of love they would answer me. Sometimes I asked myself in amazement how they could, all that time, never once offend someone like me, never once arouse jealousy and envy in someone like me. Many times I asked myself how I, a braggart and a liar, could keep from telling them about my knowledge, of which of course they had no idea, keep from wanting to astonish them with it, or even just from love for them.

They were playful and merry as children. They wandered through their beautiful groves and woods, they sang their beautiful songs, they lived on light food, the fruits of their trees, the honey of their woods and the milk of the animals that loved them. For their food and clothing they worked only a little, and lightly. They had love, and children were born to them, but I never saw in them the impulses of that cruel sensuality that comes upon almost everyone on our earth, every single one, and is the only source of almost all the sins of our humanity. They rejoiced in the children who came to them as new sharers in their bliss. There were no quarrels and no jealousy among them, and they did not even understand what these meant. Their children were everyone’s children, because they all made up one family. They had almost no illness, though there was death; but their old people died quietly, as if falling asleep, surrounded by people saying goodbye to them, blessing them, smiling at them, and themselves seen off by their bright smiles. I saw no grief or tears at this, only a love that seemed to grow to rapture, but a calm, fulfilled, contemplative rapture. One might have thought that they remained in contact with their dead even after death, and that the earthly union between them was not broken by death. They hardly understood me when I asked them about eternal life, but evidently they were so unconsciously convinced of it that it was not a question for them. They had no temples, but they had some vital, living and unbroken union with the Whole of the universe; they had no faith, but instead they had a firm knowledge that when their earthly joy had been filled to the limits of earthly nature, then there would come for them, both for the living and for the dead, a still greater widening of their contact with the Whole of the universe. They awaited that moment with joy, but without hurrying, without suffering over it, as if they already had it in the presentiments of their hearts, which they shared with one another.

In the evenings, before going to sleep, they loved to form harmonious, well-ordered choirs. In these songs they expressed all the feelings the passing day had given them; they praised it and said goodbye to it. They praised nature, the earth, the sea, the woods. They loved to make up songs about one another, and praised one another like children; these were the simplest songs, but they poured out of the heart and went to the heart. And not only in songs: it seemed that they spent their whole lives only in admiring one another. It was a kind of being in love with one another, complete and universal. Their other songs, solemn and rapturous, I hardly understood at all. Understanding the words, I could never penetrate their whole meaning. It remained, as it were, beyond the reach of my mind, but my heart seemed to be filled with it without my knowing, more and more.

I often told them that I had sensed all this long ago, that all this joy and glory had spoken to me already on our earth as a calling anguish that sometimes reached an unbearable sorrow; that I had sensed all of them and their glory in the dreams of my heart and the fancies of my mind; that I often could not look at the setting sun on our earth without tears... That in my hatred for the people of our earth there was always anguish: why can I not hate them without loving them, why can I not help forgiving them? And in my love for them there was anguish: why can I not love them without hating them? They listened to me, and I saw that they could not imagine what I was saying; but I did not regret telling them: I knew that they understood the whole force of my longing for those I had left. Yes, when they looked at me with their dear gaze filled with love, when I felt that in their presence my heart too became as innocent and truthful as theirs, then I did not regret that I did not understand them. The sense of the fullness of life took my breath away, and I silently worshipped them.

Oh, everyone laughs in my face now and assures me that one cannot see in a dream such details as I am describing now; that in my dream I saw or felt only one sensation, born of my own heart in delirium, and that I made up the details myself after I woke. And when I told them that perhaps it really was so, good God, how they laughed in my face and how I amused them! Oh yes, of course, I was overwhelmed only by the feeling of that dream, and that alone survived in my heart, wounded until it bled; but the real images and forms of my dream, the ones I really saw at the very hour of my dreaming, were filled out to such harmony, were so enchanting and beautiful, and so true, that when I woke I was of course unable to embody them in our feeble words, so that they must have blurred in my mind; and so perhaps I really was forced, without knowing it, to make up the details afterwards, and of course distorted them, especially with my passionate desire to convey them as quickly as possible, at least to some extent. But how can I not believe that it was all real? It was perhaps a thousand times better, brighter and more joyful than I am telling it. Let it be a dream, but all this could not not have been. Do you know, I will tell you a secret: perhaps it was not a dream at all! For something happened here, something so horribly true, that it could not have been dreamed. Let my heart have given birth to my dream; but could my heart alone have given birth to the terrible truth that happened to me afterwards? How could I alone have invented it, or my heart have dreamed it? Could my petty heart and my capricious, worthless mind have risen to such a revelation of the truth? Oh, judge for yourselves: I have kept it hidden until now, but now I will tell this truth too. The fact is that I... corrupted them all!

## V

Yes, yes, it ended with my corrupting them all! How it could have happened I do not know; I don’t remember clearly. The dream flew across thousands of years and left in me only a sense of the whole. I know only that I was the cause of the fall. Like a foul trichina, like the atom of a plague that infects whole countries, so I infected with myself the whole of that happy earth that had known no sin before me. They learned to lie, and came to love lies, and came to know the beauty of a lie. Oh, perhaps it began innocently, with a joke, with flirtation, with a lovers’ game, perhaps really with an atom; but that atom of a lie got into their hearts, and they liked it. Then sensuality was quickly born, sensuality gave birth to jealousy, jealousy to cruelty... Oh, I don’t know, I don’t remember; but soon, very soon, the first blood was shed. They were astonished and horrified, and began to separate and draw apart. Alliances appeared, but now against one another. Reproaches began, accusations. They came to know shame, and made shame into a virtue. The idea of honour was born, and every alliance raised its own banner. They began to torture animals, and the animals withdrew from them into the forests and became their enemies. The struggle began for separation, for isolation, for the individual, for mine and yours. They began to speak different languages. They came to know sorrow and loved sorrow; they thirsted for suffering and said that Truth is reached only through suffering. Then science appeared among them. When they became wicked, they began to talk of brotherhood and humanity, and understood these ideas. When they became criminals, they invented justice and prescribed whole codes of law for themselves to keep it, and to enforce the codes they set up the guillotine.

They barely remembered what they had lost; they did not even want to believe that they had once been innocent and happy. They even laughed at the possibility of their former happiness and called it a dream. They could not even picture it to themselves in forms and images; but, strange and wonderful thing: having lost all faith in their former happiness, having called it a fairy tale, they wanted so much to be innocent and happy again, once more, that they fell down before the desire of their heart like children, made a god of that desire, built temples and began to pray to their own idea, their own “desire”, while at the same time fully believing that it could not be fulfilled or realised, yet adoring it and worshipping it with tears. And yet, if it had been possible for them to return to the innocent and happy state they had lost, if someone had suddenly shown it to them again and asked them whether they wanted to go back to it, they would certainly have refused. They answered me: “Let us be deceitful, wicked and unjust; we know it and weep over it, and torment ourselves for it, and torture and punish ourselves more, perhaps, than even that merciful Judge who will judge us and whose name we do not know. But we have science, and through it we shall find the truth again, but this time we shall accept it consciously. Knowledge is higher than feeling; the consciousness of life is higher than life. Science will give us wisdom, wisdom will reveal the laws, and the knowledge of the laws of happiness is higher than happiness.”

That is what they said; and after such words each of them loved himself more than anyone else, and they could not have done otherwise. Each became so jealous of his own personality that he tried with all his might only to humble and diminish it in others, and made that his life. Slavery appeared, even voluntary slavery: the weak willingly submitted to the stronger, on the sole condition that the stronger would help them crush those still weaker than themselves. Righteous men appeared, who came to these people with tears and spoke to them of their pride, of their loss of measure and harmony, of their loss of shame. They were laughed at or stoned. Holy blood was shed on the thresholds of the temples. On the other hand, people began to appear who started to think up how everyone could unite again, so that each, without ceasing to love himself more than anyone else, would at the same time not interfere with anyone else, and so all would live together as if in a harmonious society. Whole wars were fought over this idea. All those at war firmly believed at the same time that science, wisdom and the instinct of self-preservation would at last force man to unite into a harmonious and rational society; and so, for the time being, to speed things up, the “wise” tried to exterminate as quickly as possible all the “unwise” who did not understand their idea, so that they would not hinder its triumph. But the instinct of self-preservation quickly began to weaken; proud and sensual men appeared who demanded outright all or nothing. To gain everything, they turned to crime, and if that failed, to suicide. Religions appeared with a cult of non-being and self-destruction for the sake of eternal peace in nothingness. At last these people grew weary of their senseless toil, and suffering appeared on their faces, and these people proclaimed that suffering is beauty, for only in suffering is there thought. They sang of suffering in their songs.

I walked among them, wringing my hands, and wept over them; but I loved them perhaps even more than before, when there was no suffering on their faces yet and they were innocent and so beautiful. I loved the earth they had defiled even more than when it had been paradise, only because grief had appeared on it. Alas, I always loved grief and sorrow, but only for myself, for myself; while for them I wept, pitying them. I stretched out my hands to them, accusing, cursing and despising myself in despair. I told them that I had done all this, I alone; that it was I who had brought them corruption, infection and lies! I begged them to crucify me on a cross; I taught them how to make a cross. I could not, I had not the strength to kill myself, but I wanted to take torments from them; I thirsted for torments, I thirsted for my blood to be shed to the last drop in those torments. But they only laughed at me and in the end began to take me for a holy fool. They justified me; they said that they had only got what they themselves wanted, and that everything that is now could not have been otherwise. At last they told me that I was becoming dangerous to them and that they would put me in a madhouse if I did not keep quiet. Then sorrow entered my soul with such force that my heart was wrung and I felt that I would die, and then... well, then I woke up.

It was already morning, that is, it was not yet light, but it was nearly six o’clock. I came to in the same easy chair; my candle had burned down completely; they were asleep at the captain’s, and all around there was a silence rare in our lodgings. The first thing I did was jump up in extreme astonishment; nothing like this had ever happened to me, down to the smallest trifles: I had never, for instance, fallen asleep like that in my chair. Then suddenly, while I was standing and coming to myself, I suddenly caught sight of my revolver, ready, loaded; but in an instant I pushed it away from me! Oh, now life, and life! I raised my hands and called out to the eternal truth; not called out, but wept; rapture, immeasurable rapture lifted my whole being. Yes, life, and preaching! I decided on preaching at that very moment, and of course for the rest of my life! I am going to preach, I want to preach. What? The truth, for I have seen it, seen it with my own eyes, seen all its glory!

And ever since then I have been preaching! Besides, I love all those who laugh at me more than all the rest. Why that is, I don’t know and can’t explain, but let it be so. They say that I am already getting confused, that is, if I am so confused already, what will happen later? It is the honest truth: I am getting confused, and perhaps it will get worse later. And of course I shall get confused several times before I find out how to preach, that is, with what words and what deeds, because it is very difficult to do. Even now I see it all as clear as day; but listen: who doesn’t get confused? And yet everyone is going towards the same thing, at least everyone is striving for the same thing, from the sage to the lowest robber, only by different roads. That is an old truth, but here is what is new: I cannot get very confused. Because I have seen the truth; I have seen and I know that people can be beautiful and happy without losing the ability to live on earth. I will not and cannot believe that evil is the normal condition of people. And yet that faith of mine is exactly what they all laugh at. But how can I not believe? I have seen the truth; not invented it with my mind, but seen it, seen it, and its living image has filled my soul forever. I saw it in such complete fullness that I cannot believe it could not exist among people. So how can I get confused? I will stray, of course, even several times, and perhaps I will even speak in other people’s words, but not for long: the living image of what I saw will always be with me, and will always correct me and set me on the right path. Oh, I am cheerful, I am fresh, I am going, going, even if it takes a thousand years. You know, at first I even wanted to hide that I had corrupted them all, but that was a mistake, the first mistake already! But the truth whispered to me that I was lying, and protected me and guided me.

But how to build paradise I do not know, because I cannot put it into words. After my dream I lost my words. At least all the main words, the most necessary ones. But never mind: I will go and keep talking, tirelessly, because I did see it with my own eyes, even if I cannot describe what I saw. But that is what the scoffers don’t understand: “He saw a dream, they say, a delusion, a hallucination.” Oh! Is that so wise? And they are so proud! A dream? What is a dream? And is our life not a dream? I will say more: let it never come true, let there never be paradise (I do understand that much!); well, I will preach all the same. And yet how simple it is: in one day, in one hour, everything would be settled at once! The main thing is to love others as yourself; that is the main thing, and that is everything, nothing else at all is needed: you would find out at once how to arrange everything. And yet this is only an old truth, which has been repeated and read a billion times, and still it has not taken root! “The consciousness of life is higher than life, the knowledge of the laws of happiness is higher than happiness”: that is what we must fight against! And I will. If only everyone wants it, everything will be arranged at once.

And that little girl, I found her... And I shall go on! I shall go on!
