## On the Friend

“There is always one too many around me,” thinks the hermit. “Always once one: in the long run that makes two!”

I and Me are always too eager in conversation: how could it be borne if there were no friend?

For the hermit the friend is always the third: the third is the cork that keeps the conversation of the two from sinking into the depths.

Ah, there are too many depths for all hermits. That is why they long so for a friend, and for his height.

Our faith in others betrays what we would like to believe in ourselves. Our longing for a friend is our betrayer.

And often with love one only wants to leap over envy. And often one attacks and makes an enemy, to hide that one can be attacked.

“At least be my enemy!” So speaks true reverence, which does not dare to ask for friendship.

If you want to have a friend, you must also be willing to wage war for him; and to wage war, you must be able to be an enemy.

You should honour the enemy even in your friend. Can you go close to your friend without going over to him?

In your friend you should have your best enemy. You should be closest to him with your heart when you resist him.

You do not want to wear any clothes in front of your friend? It is to honour your friend that you give yourself to him as you are? But for that he wishes you to the devil!

Whoever hides nothing of himself is outrageous: so much reason have you to fear nakedness! Yes, if you were gods, then you might be ashamed of your clothes!

You cannot dress up finely enough for your friend: for you should be to him an arrow and a longing for the Overman.

Have you ever seen your friend asleep, to find out what he looks like? What else is your friend’s face? It is your own face, in a rough and imperfect mirror.

Have you ever seen your friend asleep? Were you not frightened that your friend looks like that? Oh my friend, man is something that must be overcome.

The friend should be a master at guessing and keeping silent: you must not want to see everything. Your dream should tell you what your friend does while awake.

Let your pity be a guessing, so that you first know whether your friend wants pity. Perhaps what he loves in you is the unbroken eye and the look of eternity.

Let your pity for your friend hide under a hard shell; you should break a tooth on it. Then it will have its delicacy and sweetness.

Are you pure air and solitude and bread and medicine to your friend? Many a man cannot loosen his own chains, and yet he is his friend’s deliverer.

Are you a slave? Then you cannot be a friend. Are you a tyrant? Then you cannot have friends.

For far too long a slave and a tyrant were hidden in woman. That is why woman is not yet capable of friendship: she knows only love.

In woman’s love there is injustice and blindness towards everything she does not love. And even in the knowing love of woman there is still ambush and lightning and night beside the light.

Woman is not yet capable of friendship: women are still cats, and birds. Or at best cows.

Woman is not yet capable of friendship. But tell me, you men, which of you is capable of friendship?

Oh, your poverty, you men, and the meanness of your souls! As much as you give your friend, I will give even to my enemy, and will not have become poorer by it.

There is comradeship: may there be friendship!

Thus spoke Zarathustra.

## On the Thousand and One Goals

Zarathustra saw many lands and many peoples: so he discovered the good and evil of many peoples. Zarathustra found no greater power on earth than good and evil.

No people could live without first valuing; but if it wants to preserve itself, it must not value as its neighbour values.

Much that was called good by this people was called scorn and disgrace by another: so I found it. Much I found called evil here, and there decked out with purple honours.

Never did one neighbour understand another: his soul was always amazed at his neighbour’s madness and wickedness.

A tablet of good things hangs over every people. Look, it is the tablet of its overcomings; look, it is the voice of its will to power.

Praiseworthy is what it finds hard; what is indispensable and hard it calls good; and what frees it even from the deepest need, the rare, the hardest of all, that it praises as holy.

Whatever makes it rule and win and shine, to its neighbour’s horror and envy: that it counts as the high, the first, the measure, the meaning of all things.

Truly, my brother, once you have come to know a people’s need and land and sky and neighbour, you can surely guess the law of its overcomings, and why it climbs this ladder to its hope.

“You shall always be the first and stand out above the others: your jealous soul shall love no one except your friend.” This made the soul of a Greek tremble; with it he went his path of greatness.

“To speak the truth and to be good with bow and arrow”: this seemed both dear and hard to the people from whom my name comes, the name that is both dear and hard to me.

“To honour father and mother and to do their will down to the root of the soul”: this tablet of overcoming another people hung up over itself, and became powerful and eternal by it.

“To practise loyalty, and for loyalty’s sake to stake honour and blood even on evil and dangerous things”: teaching itself so, another people mastered itself, and mastering itself so, it became pregnant and heavy with great hopes.

Truly, human beings gave themselves all their good and evil. Truly, they did not take it, they did not find it, it did not fall on them as a voice from heaven.

It was man who first put values into things, in order to preserve himself; he first created a meaning for things, a human meaning! That is why he calls himself “man”, that is: the one who values.

Valuing is creating: hear it, you creators! Valuing itself is the treasure and jewel of all valued things.

Only through valuing is there value; and without valuing, the nut of existence would be hollow. Hear it, you creators!

Change of values: that is change of the creators. Whoever must be a creator always destroys.

First peoples were creators, and only later individuals; truly, the individual himself is still the youngest creation.

Peoples once hung a tablet of the good over themselves. Love that wants to rule and love that wants to obey created such tablets together.

The pleasure in the herd is older than the pleasure in the I; and as long as the good conscience is called herd, only the bad conscience says: I.

Truly, the cunning I, the loveless one, that wants its own advantage in the advantage of many: that is not the origin of the herd, but its going-under.

It was always lovers and creators who created good and evil. The fire of love glows in the names of all the virtues, and the fire of anger.

Zarathustra saw many lands and many peoples: Zarathustra found no greater power on earth than the works of those who love: “good” and “evil” is their name.

Truly, the power of this praising and blaming is a monster. Tell me, who will subdue it for me, brothers? Tell me, who will throw the fetter over the thousand necks of this beast?

There have been a thousand goals so far, for there have been a thousand peoples. Only the fetter for the thousand necks is still missing; the one goal is missing. Humanity still has no goal.

But tell me, my brothers: if humanity still lacks a goal, is it not humanity itself that is still lacking?

Thus spoke Zarathustra.

## On Love of the Neighbour

You crowd around your neighbour and have fine words for it. But I tell you: your love of your neighbour is your bad love of yourselves.

You flee to your neighbour from yourselves and would like to make a virtue of it: but I see through your “selflessness”.

The You is older than the I; the You has been declared holy, but not yet the I: so man crowds towards his neighbour.

Do I advise you to love your neighbour? Rather I advise you to flee your neighbour and to love the farthest!

Higher than love of the neighbour is love of the farthest and of what is to come; higher still than love of people is love of things and of ghosts.

This ghost that runs ahead of you, my brother, is more beautiful than you; why do you not give it your flesh and your bones? But you are afraid, and run to your neighbour.

You cannot bear yourselves and do not love yourselves enough: now you want to seduce your neighbour into love, and gild yourselves with his mistake.

I wish you could not bear all kinds of neighbours and their neighbours; then you would have to create your friend and his overflowing heart out of yourselves.

You invite a witness when you want to speak well of yourselves; and when you have seduced him into thinking well of you, you think well of yourselves.

Not only he lies who speaks against what he knows, but even more he who speaks against what he does not know. And so you speak of yourselves in company, and lie to your neighbour with yourselves.

So says the fool: “Mixing with people spoils the character, especially if one has none.”

One goes to his neighbour because he is looking for himself, and another because he would like to lose himself. Your bad love of yourselves makes your solitude a prison.

It is those farther away who pay for your love of the neighbour; and as soon as there are five of you together, a sixth must always die.

I do not love your festivals either: I found too many actors there, and even the spectators often behaved like actors.

I do not teach you the neighbour, but the friend. Let the friend be the festival of the earth to you, and a foretaste of the Overman.

I teach you the friend and his overflowing heart. But one must know how to be a sponge if one wants to be loved by overflowing hearts.

I teach you the friend in whom the world stands complete, a bowl of the good: the creating friend, who always has a complete world to give away.

And as the world rolled apart for him, so it rolls together again for him in rings, as the becoming of good through evil, as the becoming of purposes out of chance.

Let the future and the farthest be the cause of your today: in your friend you should love the Overman as your cause.

My brothers, I do not advise you to love your neighbour: I advise you to love the farthest.

Thus spoke Zarathustra.

## On the Way of the Creator

Do you want, my brother, to go into solitude? Do you want to seek the way to yourself? Wait a little longer, and listen to me.

“Whoever seeks easily gets lost himself. All going off alone is guilt”: so says the herd. And you belonged to the herd for a long time.

The voice of the herd will still sound in you too. And when you say, “I no longer share one conscience with you,” it will be a lament and a pain.

Look, this pain itself was born of that one conscience; and the last glimmer of that conscience still glows on your gloom.

But you want to go the way of your gloom, which is the way to yourself? Then show me your right and your strength for it!

Are you a new strength and a new right? A first movement? A wheel rolling out of itself? Can you also force stars to turn around you?

Ah, there is so much lusting for the heights! There are so many convulsions of the ambitious! Show me that you are not one of the lustful and ambitious!

Ah, there are so many great thoughts that do no more than a bellows: they puff up and make emptier.

You call yourself free? I want to hear your ruling thought, and not that you have escaped from a yoke.

Are you one who was allowed to escape a yoke? There are many who threw away their last value when they threw away their servitude.

Free from what? What does Zarathustra care! But your eye should tell me clearly: free for what?

Can you give yourself your own evil and your own good, and hang your will over yourself like a law? Can you be your own judge, and avenger of your law?

It is terrible to be alone with the judge and avenger of one’s own law. So a star is thrown out into empty space and into the icy breath of being alone.

Today you still suffer from the many, you single one; today you still have your courage whole, and your hopes.

But one day solitude will make you tired; one day your pride will bend and your courage grind its teeth. One day you will cry out, “I am alone!”

One day you will no longer see what is high in you, and see what is low in you all too near; your very sublimity will frighten you like a ghost. One day you will cry out, “Everything is false!”

There are feelings that want to kill the solitary; if they do not succeed, well, then they must die themselves! But are you able to be a murderer?

Do you already know, my brother, the word “contempt”? And the torment of your justice, to be just to those who despise you?

You force many to change their minds about you; they hold that hard against you. You came near them and yet went past: that they will never forgive you.

You go beyond them; but the higher you climb, the smaller the eye of envy sees you. But the one who flies is hated most.

“How could you be just to me!” you must say. “I choose your injustice as the share measured out to me.”

They throw injustice and dirt at the solitary; but, my brother, if you want to be a star, you must not shine any less for them because of it!

And beware of the good and the just! They like to crucify those who invent their own virtue; they hate the solitary.

Beware too of holy simplicity! Everything that is not simple is unholy to it; it also likes to play with fire, the fire of the stake.

And beware too of the fits of your love! The solitary holds out his hand too quickly to whoever he meets.

To many people you must not give your hand, but only your paw; and I want your paw to have claws too.

But the worst enemy you can meet will always be yourself; you lie in wait for yourself in caves and forests.

Solitary one, you are going the way to yourself! And your way leads past yourself and your seven devils!

You will be heretic to yourself, and witch and fortune-teller and fool and doubter and unholy one and villain.

You must want to burn yourself in your own flame: how could you become new if you had not first become ashes!

Solitary one, you are going the way of the creator: you want to create a god for yourself out of your seven devils!

Solitary one, you are going the way of the lover: you love yourself, and that is why you despise yourself, as only lovers despise.

The lover wants to create, because he despises! What does he know of love who did not have to despise precisely what he loved!

Go into your solitude with your love and with your creating, my brother; and only late will justice limp after you.

Go into your solitude with my tears, my brother. I love him who wants to create beyond himself, and so goes under.

Thus spoke Zarathustra.

## On Little Old and Young Women

“Why do you creep so shyly through the twilight, Zarathustra? And what are you hiding so carefully under your cloak?

Is it a treasure someone has given you? Or a child that has been born to you? Or are you now going the ways of thieves yourself, you friend of the wicked?”

Truly, my brother, said Zarathustra, it is a treasure that was given me: it is a little truth that I am carrying.

But it is unruly, like a young child; and if I do not hold its mouth, it screams too loud.

As I went my way alone today, at the hour when the sun goes down, I met a little old woman, and she spoke thus to my soul:

“Zarathustra has said many things to us women too, but he has never spoken to us about woman.”

And I answered her: “About woman one should speak only to men.”

“Speak to me too about woman,” she said; “I am old enough to forget it again straight away.”

And I gave in to the little old woman, and spoke thus to her:

Everything about woman is a riddle, and everything about woman has one answer: it is called pregnancy.

For woman, man is a means: the end is always the child. But what is woman for man?

The true man wants two things: danger and play. That is why he wants woman, as the most dangerous plaything.

Man should be brought up for war, and woman for the recreation of the warrior: all else is folly.

Fruits that are too sweet the warrior does not like. That is why he likes woman; even the sweetest woman is still bitter.

Woman understands children better than man does, but man is more childlike than woman.

In the true man a child is hidden: it wants to play. Come, you women, discover the child in man for me!

Let woman be a plaything, pure and fine, like a precious stone, lit by the virtues of a world that is not yet here.

Let the ray of a star shine in your love! Let your hope be: “May I give birth to the Overman!”

Let there be bravery in your love! With your love you should go for the one who makes you afraid!

Let your honour be in your love! Otherwise woman understands little about honour. But let this be your honour: always to love more than you are loved, and never to be second.

Let man be afraid of woman when she loves: then she makes every sacrifice, and every other thing seems worthless to her.

Let man be afraid of woman when she hates: for man is only wicked at the bottom of his soul, but woman is bad there.

Whom does woman hate most? So spoke the iron to the magnet: “I hate you most, because you attract me but are not strong enough to pull me to you.”

The happiness of man is: I will. The happiness of woman is: he wills.

“Look, now the world has just become perfect!” So thinks every woman when she obeys out of complete love.

And woman must obey, and find a depth for her surface. Woman’s soul is surface, a restless, stormy film on shallow water.

But man’s soul is deep; its current roars in underground caves: woman senses its strength, but does not understand it.

Then the little old woman answered me: “Zarathustra has said many nice things, especially for those who are young enough for them.

It is strange: Zarathustra knows little of women, and yet he is right about them! Does this happen because with woman nothing is impossible?

And now take a little truth as thanks! I am old enough for it, after all!

Wrap it up and hold its mouth: otherwise it will scream too loud, this little truth.”

“Give me, woman, your little truth!” I said. And thus spoke the little old woman:

“You are going to women? Do not forget the whip!”

Thus spoke Zarathustra.

## On the Bite of the Adder

One day Zarathustra had fallen asleep under a fig tree, for it was hot, and had laid his arms over his face. Then an adder came and bit him in the neck, so that Zarathustra cried out with pain. When he had taken his arm from his face, he looked at the snake; and it recognised Zarathustra’s eyes, writhed awkwardly, and tried to get away. “Not so,” said Zarathustra; “you have not yet accepted my thanks! You woke me in time; my way is still long.” “Your way is short now,” said the adder sadly; “my poison kills.” Zarathustra smiled. “When did a dragon ever die of a snake’s poison?” he said. “But take your poison back! You are not rich enough to give it to me.” Then the adder fell on his neck again, and licked his wound.

When Zarathustra once told this to his disciples, they asked: “And what, O Zarathustra, is the moral of your story?” Zarathustra answered thus:

The good and the just call me the destroyer of morals: my story is immoral.

But if you have an enemy, do not repay him evil with good: for that would shame him. Rather prove that he has done you some good.

And rather be angry than put him to shame! And when you are cursed, I do not like it that you then want to bless. Rather curse back a little!

And if a great wrong has been done to you, then quickly do five small ones besides! It is horrible to see the man who is weighed down by wrong alone.

Did you know this already? Wrong shared is half right. And let him take the wrong upon himself who can bear it!

A little revenge is more human than no revenge at all. And if the punishment is not also a right and an honour for the one who broke the law, I do not like your punishing either.

It is nobler to put yourself in the wrong than to keep the right, especially when you are right. Only one must be rich enough for it.

I do not like your cold justice; and from the eye of your judges the executioner and his cold steel always look out at me.

Tell me, where is the justice to be found that is love with seeing eyes?

Then invent for me the love that bears not only all punishment but also all guilt!

Then invent for me the justice that acquits everyone, except the one who judges!

Do you want to hear this too? In the one who wants to be just through and through, even lying becomes kindness to people.

But how could I want to be just through and through! How can I give everyone his own! Let this be enough for me: I give everyone my own.

Finally, my brothers, beware of doing wrong to any hermit! How could a hermit forget! How could he repay!

A hermit is like a deep well. It is easy to throw a stone in; but once it has sunk to the bottom, tell me, who will bring it out again?

Beware of insulting the hermit! But if you have done it, well then, kill him too!

Thus spoke Zarathustra.
