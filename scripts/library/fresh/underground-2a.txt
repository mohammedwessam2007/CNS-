## IX

Gentlemen, I am joking, of course, and I know myself that my jokes are not good ones; but you cannot take everything as a joke. Perhaps I am joking through clenched teeth. Gentlemen, questions are tormenting me; answer them for me. Here you are, for example, wanting to wean man from his old habits and correct his will in accordance with the demands of science and common sense. But how do you know that man not only can but must be remade like this? What makes you conclude that human wanting so badly needs correcting? In a word, how do you know that such a correction will really bring man any advantage? And, to say it all, why are you so certainly convinced that not going against our real, normal advantages, guaranteed by the arguments of reason and arithmetic, really is always advantageous for man, and is a law for all mankind? For the time being that is only your assumption. Suppose it is a law of logic; it may not be a law of mankind at all. Perhaps you think, gentlemen, that I am mad? Allow me to explain. I agree: man is above all a creative animal, condemned to strive consciously towards a goal and to practise the art of engineering, that is, eternally and ceaselessly to build himself a road, wherever it may lead. But perhaps that is exactly why he sometimes wants to swerve aside: because he is condemned to build this road; and perhaps also because, however stupid the spontaneous man of action may be in general, it sometimes still occurs to him that the road, it turns out, almost always leads somewhere or other, and that the main thing is not where it leads, but that it should keep going, and that the well-behaved child, neglecting the art of engineering, should not give himself up to ruinous idleness, which, as everyone knows, is the mother of all vices. Man loves to create and to build roads, that is beyond dispute. But why does he also passionately love destruction and chaos? Tell me that! But about this I want to say a few words separately myself. Isn’t it perhaps because he is instinctively afraid of reaching his goal and completing the building he is creating that he loves destruction and chaos so much (for it is beyond dispute that he sometimes loves them very much; that is just how it is)? How do you know: perhaps he loves the building only from a distance and not at all close up; perhaps he only loves building it and not living in it, leaving it afterwards *aux animaux domestiques*, to the domestic animals, such as ants, sheep and so on and so on. Now ants have quite different tastes. They have one amazing building of this kind, forever indestructible: the anthill.

With the anthill the worthy ants began, and with the anthill they will surely end, which does great credit to their constancy and their positive outlook. But man is a frivolous and unseemly creature, and perhaps, like a chess player, he loves only the process of reaching the goal, and not the goal itself. And who knows (one cannot vouch for it), perhaps the whole goal on earth towards which mankind is striving lies only in this endless process of getting there, in other words, in life itself, and not really in the goal, which of course must be nothing but two times two makes four, that is, a formula; and two times two makes four, gentlemen, is no longer life, but the beginning of death. At any rate, man has always somehow been afraid of this two times two makes four, and I am afraid of it even now. Suppose man does nothing but search for this two times two makes four; he sails the oceans, he sacrifices his life in this search; but to find it, to really find it, by God, he is somehow afraid. For he feels that once he has found it, there will be nothing left to search for. Workmen, when they have finished their work, at least get their money, go to the tavern, then end up at the police station, and there is a week’s occupation for you. But where will man go? At any rate, each time he reaches such goals you notice something awkward in him. He loves getting there, but having got there, not quite; and that, of course, is terribly funny. In a word, man is made comically; there is obviously a pun in all this. But two times two makes four is still a most insufferable thing. Two times two makes four is, in my opinion, nothing but a piece of insolence, sir. Two times two makes four stands there like a fop with hands on hips, blocks your road and spits. I agree that two times two makes four is an excellent thing; but if we are going to praise everything, then two times two makes five is sometimes a very charming little thing too.

And why are you so firmly, so solemnly sure that only the normal and the positive, in a word, only well-being, is to man’s advantage? Isn’t reason mistaken about advantages? Perhaps man loves something besides well-being? Perhaps he loves suffering just as much? Perhaps suffering is exactly as much to his advantage as well-being? And man does sometimes love suffering terribly, passionately, and that is a fact. There is no need to consult world history here; ask yourself, if only you are a man and have lived at all. As for my personal opinion, to love only well-being is even somehow indecent. Whether it is good or bad, smashing something now and then is also very pleasant. I am not really standing up here for suffering, or for well-being either. I am standing up for... my caprice, and for its being guaranteed to me when I need it. Suffering, for example, is not allowed in light comedies, I know that. In the crystal palace it is unthinkable: suffering is doubt, is denial, and what kind of crystal palace is it if you can doubt it? And yet I am sure that man will never give up real suffering, that is, destruction and chaos. Suffering: why, it is the sole cause of consciousness. Though I said at the beginning that consciousness, in my view, is man’s greatest misfortune, I know that man loves it and will not exchange it for any satisfactions. Consciousness, for example, is infinitely higher than two times two. After two times two, of course, there will be nothing left, not only to do, but even to find out. All you will be able to do then is stop up your five senses and sink into contemplation. Well, with consciousness the result is the same, that is, there will also be nothing to do; but at least you can sometimes give yourself a good whipping, and that livens you up, at any rate. It may be reactionary, but it is still better than nothing.
