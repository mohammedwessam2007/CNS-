## On Bliss Against One’s Will

With such riddles and bitterness in his heart Zarathustra crossed the sea. But when he was four days’ journey away from the blessed isles and from his friends, he had overcome all his pain: victorious and with firm feet he stood on his fate again. And then Zarathustra spoke thus to his rejoicing conscience:

“I am alone again, and I want to be, alone with the clear sky and the open sea; and again it is afternoon around me.

It was in the afternoon that I first found my friends, and in the afternoon the second time too: at the hour when all light grows stiller.

For whatever happiness is still on its way between heaven and earth now looks for a bright soul to lodge in: all light has now grown stiller with happiness.

O afternoon of my life! Once my happiness too went down into the valley to look for a lodging: then it found these open, hospitable souls.

O afternoon of my life! What did I not give up to have one thing: this living plantation of my thoughts, and this morning light of my highest hope!

The creator once looked for companions and children of his hope: and look, it turned out that he could not find them, unless he first created them himself.

So I am in the middle of my work, going to my children and returning from them: for his children’s sake Zarathustra must complete himself.

For from the bottom of the heart one loves only one’s child and one’s work; and where there is great love of oneself, it is the sign of pregnancy: so I have found.

My children are still green in their first spring, standing close together and shaken together by the winds, the trees of my garden and of my best soil.

And truly! Where such trees stand together, there are blessed isles!

But one day I will dig them up and set each one alone, so that it learns solitude and defiance and caution.

Gnarled and bent and with supple hardness it shall then stand by the sea, a living lighthouse of unconquerable life.

There, where the storms plunge down into the sea, and the mountain’s trunk drinks water, each shall one day keep his day and night watches, for his testing and his knowledge.

He shall be known and tested, to see whether he is of my kind and descent; whether he is master of a long will, silent even when he speaks, and yielding in such a way that in giving he takes:

so that one day he may become my companion, and a fellow creator and fellow celebrant of Zarathustra: one who writes my will on my tablets, for the fuller completion of all things.

And for his sake and for the sake of those like him, I must complete myself: that is why I now avoid my happiness and offer myself to every unhappiness, for my last testing and knowledge.

And truly, it was time for me to go; and the wanderer’s shadow and the longest boredom and the stillest hour, all said to me: ‘It is high time!’

The wind blew through my keyhole and said ‘Come!’ The door sprang cunningly open and said ‘Go!’

But I lay chained to my love for my children: desire laid this snare for me, the desire for love, that I should become my children’s prey and lose myself in them.

To desire: for me that already means to have lost myself. I have you, my children! In this having, everything shall be certainty and nothing desire.

But the sun of my love lay brooding on me; Zarathustra was cooking in his own juice; then shadows and doubts flew over me.

I was already longing for frost and winter: ‘Oh, that frost and winter would make me crack and crunch again!’ I sighed; then icy mists rose out of me.

My past burst its graves; many a pain buried alive woke up: it had only been sleeping, hidden in the clothes of the dead.

So everything called to me in signs: ‘It is time!’ But I did not hear: until at last my abyss stirred and my thought bit me.

Ah, abysmal thought, you who are my thought! When shall I find the strength to hear you digging and no longer tremble?

My heart pounds up to my throat when I hear you digging! Even your silence wants to strangle me, you abysmally silent one!

I have never yet dared to call you up: it was enough that I carried you with me! I was not yet strong enough for the lion’s final high spirits and wilfulness.

Your heaviness has always been terrible enough for me: but one day I shall yet find the strength and the lion’s voice that calls you up!

When I have overcome myself in that, then I will overcome myself in something greater too; and a victory shall be the seal of my completion!

Meanwhile I am still drifting on uncertain seas; chance flatters me, the smooth-tongued one; I look forward and backward, and still I see no end.

The hour of my last struggle has not yet come for me; or is it coming to me just now? Truly, sea and life look at me all around with treacherous beauty!

O afternoon of my life! O happiness before evening! O harbour on the high seas! O peace in uncertainty! How I mistrust you all!

Truly, I am mistrustful of your treacherous beauty! I am like the lover who mistrusts a smile that is too velvety.

As the jealous one pushes his beloved away from him, tender even in his harshness, so I push this blissful hour away from me.

Away with you, you blissful hour! With you there came to me a bliss against my will! Here I stand, willing for my deepest pain: you came at the wrong time!

Away with you, you blissful hour! Rather take lodging there, with my children! Hurry! And bless them before evening with my happiness!

There, evening is already near: the sun is sinking. Away, my happiness!”

Thus spoke Zarathustra. And he waited for his unhappiness all night: but he waited in vain. The night stayed clear and still, and happiness itself came nearer and nearer to him. But towards morning Zarathustra laughed to his heart and said mockingly: “Happiness is running after me. That is because I do not run after women. But happiness is a woman.”

## Before Sunrise

O sky above me, you pure one! You deep one! You abyss of light! Looking at you I shudder with divine desires.

To throw myself into your height: that is my depth! To hide myself in your purity: that is my innocence!

The god is veiled by his beauty: so you hide your stars. You do not speak: so you proclaim your wisdom to me.

Silent over the roaring sea you have risen for me today; your love and your modesty speak revelation to my roaring soul.

That you came to me beautiful, veiled in your beauty; that you speak to me silently, revealed in your wisdom:

oh, how could I not guess all the modesty of your soul! You came to me before the sun, to me, the loneliest one.

We have been friends from the beginning: grief and dread and ground we have in common; even the sun we have in common.

We do not speak to each other, because we know too much: we are silent to each other, we smile our knowledge to each other.

Are you not the light to my fire? Have you not the sister-soul to my insight?

Together we learned everything; together we learned to climb above ourselves to ourselves, and to smile without clouds:

to smile down without clouds from bright eyes and from miles away, when beneath us compulsion and purpose and guilt steam like rain.

And when I wandered alone, for whom did my soul hunger in the nights and on wrong paths? And when I climbed mountains, whom did I ever look for on mountains, if not you?

And all my wandering and mountain climbing: it was only a necessity and a makeshift of the clumsy one. My whole will wants only to fly, to fly into you!

And whom did I hate more than passing clouds and everything that stains you? And I hated even my own hatred, because it stained you!

I resent the passing clouds, these creeping beasts of prey: they take from you and me what we have in common, the tremendous, unbounded saying of Yes and Amen.

We resent these go-betweens and mixers, the passing clouds: these half-and-halfs, who have learned neither to bless nor to curse from the heart.

I would rather sit in a barrel under a closed sky, rather sit in the abyss without a sky, than see you, sky of light, stained with passing clouds!

And often I longed to pin them down with jagged golden wires of lightning, so that, like the thunder, I might beat the drum on their kettle-bellies:

an angry drummer, because they rob me of your Yes! and Amen!, you sky above me, you pure one! you bright one! you abyss of light! because they rob you of my Yes! and Amen!

For I would rather have noise and thunder and the curses of storms than this cautious, doubting cat-like calm; and among men too I hate most all soft-steppers and half-and-halfs and doubting, hesitating passing clouds.

And “whoever cannot bless shall learn to curse!”: this clear teaching fell to me from a clear sky; this star still stands in my sky even on black nights.

But I am one who blesses and one who says Yes, if only you are around me, you pure one! you bright one! you abyss of light! Then I carry my blessing Yes into all abysses.

I have become one who blesses and one who says Yes: and for that I wrestled long and was a wrestler, so that one day I might get my hands free for blessing.

But this is my blessing: to stand over every single thing as its own sky, as its round roof, its azure bell and eternal security: and blessed is he who blesses thus!

For all things are baptised at the well of eternity and beyond good and evil; but good and evil themselves are only shadows in between, and damp afflictions, and passing clouds.

Truly, it is a blessing and not a blasphemy when I teach: “Over all things stand the sky Chance, the sky Innocence, the sky Accident, the sky High Spirits.”

“By accident”: that is the oldest nobility in the world; I gave it back to all things; I set them free from slavery under purpose.

This freedom and heavenly cheerfulness I set like an azure bell over all things, when I taught that over them and through them no “eternal will” wills.

This high spirits and this folly I set in the place of that will, when I taught: “In everything one thing is impossible: rationality!”

A little reason, it is true, a seed of wisdom scattered from star to star: this leaven is mixed into all things; for the sake of folly, wisdom is mixed into all things!

A little wisdom is possible, certainly; but this blessed certainty I found in all things: that they would rather dance on the feet of chance.

O sky above me, you pure one! you high one! This is now your purity to me, that there is no eternal spider of reason and no spider’s web of reason:

that you are a dance floor for divine chances, that you are a gods’ table for divine dice and dice-players!

But you blush? Did I say what may not be said? Did I blaspheme when I wanted to bless you?

Or is it the shame of being two that made you blush? Do you bid me go and be silent, because now the day is coming?

The world is deep: and deeper than the day has ever thought. Not everything may have words before the day. But the day is coming: so let us part now!

O sky above me, you modest one! you glowing one! O you my happiness before sunrise! The day is coming: so let us part now!

Thus spoke Zarathustra.
