## X

You believe in a crystal building, forever indestructible, that is, one at which you can’t stick out your tongue on the sly or make a rude sign in your pocket. Well, and perhaps I am afraid of this building precisely because it is crystal and forever indestructible, and because you won’t be able to stick out your tongue at it even on the sly.

You see: if instead of a palace there were a henhouse, and it started to rain, I might climb into the henhouse so as not to get wet; but all the same I would not take the henhouse for a palace out of gratitude for its keeping me out of the rain. You laugh; you even say that in that case a henhouse and a mansion are all the same. Yes, I answer, if one had to live only so as not to get wet.

But what is to be done if I have taken it into my head that one does not live for that alone, and that if one is going to live at all, one should live in a mansion? That is my wanting, that is my desire. You will scrape it out of me only when you have changed my desires. Well, change them, then; tempt me with something else, give me another ideal. But in the meantime I will not take a henhouse for a palace. Let it even be that the crystal building is a fantasy, that by the laws of nature it is not supposed to exist, and that I invented it only out of my own stupidity, out of certain old irrational habits of our generation. But what do I care that it is not supposed to exist? Isn’t it all the same, if it exists in my desires, or rather exists as long as my desires exist? Perhaps you are laughing again? Laugh away; I will accept all your mockery, and still I will not say that I am full when I am hungry; still I know that I will not settle for a compromise, for an endlessly recurring zero, simply because it exists according to the laws of nature and really does exist. I will not accept as the crown of my desires a big block of flats, with apartments for poor tenants on thousand-year leases, and, just in case, a dentist’s sign out front. Destroy my desires, wipe out my ideals, show me something better, and I will follow you. You may say it isn’t worth getting involved with me; but in that case I can give you the same answer. We are reasoning seriously; and if you don’t want to honour me with your attention, I won’t bow and beg. I have my underground.

And as long as I am still alive and still want things, may my hand wither if I carry even one little brick to such a block of flats! Never mind that just now I myself rejected the crystal building for the sole reason that you would not be able to tease it with your tongue. I did not say that at all because I am so fond of sticking out my tongue. Perhaps what made me angry was only that among all your buildings there is not yet a single one at which one would not want to stick out one’s tongue. On the contrary, I would let my tongue be cut off altogether, out of sheer gratitude, if only things could be arranged so that I myself would never want to stick it out again. What do I care that it can’t be arranged, and that one has to be content with apartments? Then why am I made with such desires? Can it be that I am made this way only to reach the conclusion that my whole make-up is nothing but a swindle? Can that be the whole purpose? I don’t believe it.

But do you know what? I am convinced that people like us, from the underground, need to be kept on a short leash. Though we are capable of sitting silent in the underground for forty years, once we come out into the light and break loose, we talk and talk and talk...

## XI

The long and the short of it, gentlemen: it is better to do nothing! Better conscious inertia! And so, long live the underground! Though I said that I envy the normal man to the last drop of my bile, I don’t want to be him on the terms on which I see him (though I won’t stop envying him all the same. No, no, the underground is more advantageous in any case!). There, at least, one can... Oh, but I am lying even here! I am lying because I know myself, as surely as two times two, that it is not the underground that is better at all, but something else, something completely different, which I thirst for but can never find! To the devil with the underground!

Here is what would even be better: if I myself believed even a little of everything I have just written. I swear to you, gentlemen, that I do not believe one single word, not a single little word, of what I have just scribbled! That is, perhaps I do believe it, but at the same time, for some unknown reason, I feel and suspect that I am lying like a cobbler.

“Then why did you write all this?” you say to me.

Well, suppose I put you away for forty years with nothing to do, and then came to see you in the underground after forty years, to find out what state you had reached? Can a man really be left alone without anything to do for forty years?

“And this isn’t shameful, this isn’t humiliating!” you will perhaps say to me, shaking your heads contemptuously. “You thirst for life, yet you settle the questions of life yourself with a tangle of logic. And how tiresome, how insolent your antics are, and at the same time how frightened you are! You talk nonsense and you are pleased with it; you say insolent things, and yet you are constantly afraid of them and keep apologising. You assure us that you are afraid of nothing, and at the same time you fish for our good opinion. You assure us that you are grinding your teeth, and at the same time you crack jokes to make us laugh. You know that your witticisms are not witty, but you are obviously very pleased with their literary merit. Perhaps you really have suffered, but you have no respect at all for your own suffering. There is truth in you, but there is no purity in you; out of the pettiest vanity you carry your truth out for show, for shame, to the market... You really do want to say something, but out of fear you hide your last word, because you don’t have the resolve to say it, only a cowardly insolence. You boast of consciousness, but you only waver, because although your mind is working, your heart is darkened by corruption, and without a pure heart there can be no full, right consciousness. And how tiresome you are, how you push yourself on us, how you posture! Lies, lies and lies!”

Of course, I made up all these words of yours myself just now. That too comes from the underground. For forty years on end I listened to these words of yours through a crack. I invented them myself; after all, that was all there was to invent. No wonder I learned them by heart and they took on a literary form...

But can it be, can it really be, that you are so gullible as to imagine that I will print all this, and give it to you to read, besides? And here is another puzzle for me: why do I call you “gentlemen”, why do I address you as if you really were my readers? Confessions like the ones I intend to start setting down are not printed and are not given to others to read. At least, I don’t have that much firmness in me, and I don’t think it necessary to have it. But you see, a fancy has come into my head, and I want to carry it out, whatever it costs. Here is the thing.

There are things in every man’s memories that he does not reveal to everyone, but only to his friends. There are others that he will not reveal even to his friends, but only to himself, and even then in secret. But there are, finally, things that a man is afraid to reveal even to himself, and every decent man has piled up a good number of such things. That is, it is even like this: the more decent the man, the more of them he has. At least, I myself have only recently decided to remember some of my earlier adventures; until now I have always avoided them, even with a kind of uneasiness. But now, when I am not only remembering them but have even decided to write them down, now I want precisely to test this: can a man be completely honest at least with himself, and not be afraid of the whole truth? I will note, by the way, that Heine claims that true autobiographies are almost impossible, and that a man is sure to lie about himself. In his opinion Rousseau, for example, certainly lied about himself in his Confessions, and even lied deliberately, out of vanity. I am sure Heine is right; I understand very well how one can sometimes slander oneself with whole crimes purely out of vanity, and I even grasp very well what kind of vanity that may be. But Heine was judging a man who confessed before the public. I, however, am writing for myself alone, and I declare once and for all that if I write as if addressing readers, it is only for show, because it is easier for me to write that way. It is a form, an empty form; I shall never have any readers. I have already said so...

I do not want to be restricted by anything in editing my notes. I will not introduce any order or system. Whatever I remember, I will write down.

Well, for example: someone might seize on my words and ask me: if you really are not counting on readers, then why do you now make such agreements with yourself, and on paper too, saying that you won’t introduce order or system, that you will write down whatever you remember, and so on and so on? Why are you explaining yourself? Why are you apologising?

“Well, there you are,” I answer.

There is a whole psychology in this, however. Perhaps it is simply that I am a coward. And perhaps it is that I deliberately imagine an audience in front of me so as to behave more decently while I am writing. There may be a thousand reasons.

But here is something else: why, for what purpose exactly, do I want to write? If not for the public, then surely I could just remember everything in my head, without putting it on paper?

Quite so, sir; but on paper it will come out somehow more solemn. There is something impressive in it; I will judge myself more strictly, and the style will improve. Besides, perhaps writing things down really will bring me relief. Today, for example, one memory from long ago weighs on me especially. It came back to me clearly a few days ago, and since then it has stayed with me like an annoying tune that won’t go away. And yet I must get rid of it. I have hundreds of such memories; but from time to time one out of the hundred stands out and weighs on me. For some reason I believe that if I write it down, it will leave me. Why not try?

Finally: I am bored, and I am always doing nothing. And writing really is a kind of work. They say work makes a man good and honest. Well, here is a chance, at least.

Today it is snowing, an almost wet snow, yellow and murky. It snowed yesterday too, and a few days ago as well. I think it was because of the wet snow that I remembered the story that now won’t leave me alone. So let this be a story apropos of the wet snow.
