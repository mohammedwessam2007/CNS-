## On Old and New Tablets

## 1

Here I sit and wait, old broken tablets around me, and new tablets too, half written. When will my hour come?

The hour of my going down, my going under: for I want to go to men once more.

That is what I am waiting for now: for first the signs must come to me that it is my hour: namely, the laughing lion with the flock of doves.

Meanwhile I talk to myself, as one who has time. No one tells me anything new: so I tell myself myself.

## 2

When I came to men, I found them sitting on an old conceit: they all thought they had long known what is good and evil for man.

All talk of virtue seemed to them an old, tired thing; and whoever wanted to sleep well still talked of “good” and “evil” before going to bed.

I disturbed this sleepiness when I taught: what is good and evil, no one yet knows, unless it is the creator!

But that is the one who creates man’s goal and gives the earth its meaning and its future: only he makes it so that something is good and evil.

And I bade them overturn their old professorial chairs, and wherever that old conceit had sat; I bade them laugh at their great masters of virtue and saints and poets and saviours of the world.

I bade them laugh at their gloomy sages, and at whoever had ever sat as a black scarecrow, warning, on the tree of life.

I sat down by their great avenue of graves, and even beside carrion and vultures; and I laughed at all their past and its crumbling, decaying glory.

Truly, like preachers of repentance and fools I raised a cry of anger and outrage over all their great and small: that their best is so very small! That their worst is so very small! So I laughed.

So my wise longing cried and laughed out of me, the longing that was born on mountains, a wild wisdom truly! my great longing with its rushing wings.

And often it carried me away, and up, and off, in the middle of laughter: then I flew, shuddering, an arrow, through sun-drunk rapture:

out into distant futures that no dream has yet seen, into hotter souths than any artist ever dreamed: there, where gods dancing are ashamed of all clothes:

so that I speak in parables, and limp and stammer like poets; and truly, I am ashamed that I must still be a poet!

where all becoming seemed to me the dance of gods and the wantonness of gods, and the world let loose and unbridled and fleeing back to itself:

as an eternal fleeing from and seeking again of many gods, as the blessed contradicting of themselves, hearing themselves again, belonging to themselves again, of many gods:

where all time seemed to me a blessed mockery of moments, where necessity was freedom itself, playing blissfully with the sting of freedom:

where I also found my old devil and arch-enemy again, the spirit of gravity, and everything he created: compulsion, statute, need and consequence and purpose and will and good and evil:

for must there not be something that is danced over, danced beyond? Must there not be moles and heavy dwarfs, for the sake of the light, the lightest?

## 3

It was there too that I picked up the word “Overman” from the road, and that man is something that must be overcome:

that man is a bridge and not an end: calling himself blessed for his noon and his evening, as the way to new dawns:

the Zarathustra-word of the great noon, and whatever else I hung up over man like purple second sunsets.

Truly, I also let them see new stars, together with new nights; and over clouds and day and night I spread laughter like a bright tent.

I taught them all my poetry and striving: to compose into one and bring together what is fragment in man and riddle and dreadful accident;

as poet, guesser of riddles and redeemer of chance, I taught them to work on the future, and by creating to redeem all that was.

To redeem what is past in man and to transform every “It was”, until the will says: “But thus I willed it! Thus shall I will it”:

this I called redemption to them; this alone I taught them to call redemption.

Now I wait for my own redemption: that I may go to them for the last time.

For I want to go to men once more: among them I want to go under; dying, I want to give them my richest gift!

I learned this from the sun when it goes down, the over-rich one: then it pours gold into the sea out of inexhaustible riches,

so that even the poorest fisherman rows with golden oars! For I once saw this, and could not have my fill of tears as I watched.

Like the sun, Zarathustra too wants to go under: now he sits here and waits, old broken tablets around him, and new tablets too, half written.

## 4

Look, here is a new tablet: but where are my brothers to carry it with me down into the valley and into hearts of flesh?

So my great love of the farthest demands: do not spare your neighbour! Man is something that must be overcome.

There are many ways and means of overcoming: see to that yourself! But only a buffoon thinks: “Man can also be jumped over.”

Overcome yourself even in your neighbour: and a right that you can seize for yourself, you should not let anyone give you!

What you do, no one can do back to you. Look, there is no repayment.

Whoever cannot command himself should obey. And many can command themselves, but much is still lacking before they also obey themselves!

## 5

This is the way of noble souls: they want nothing for nothing, least of all life.

Whoever is of the mob wants to live for nothing; but we others, to whom life gave itself, we always think about what we can best give in return!

And truly, it is a noble saying that says: “What life promises us, we will keep for life!”

One should not want to enjoy where one does not give enjoyment. And one should not want to enjoy!

For enjoyment and innocence are the most bashful things: neither wants to be sought. One should have them; but one should rather seek guilt and pain!

## 6

O my brothers, whoever is a first-born is always sacrificed. Now we are first-born.

We all bleed on secret sacrificial altars; we all burn and roast in honour of old idols.

Our best is still young: that tempts old palates. Our flesh is tender, our hide only a lamb’s hide: how could we not tempt old idol-priests!

Within ourselves he still lives, the old idol-priest, who roasts our best for his feast. Ah, my brothers, how could the first-born not be sacrifices!

But so our nature wills it; and I love those who do not want to preserve themselves. Those who go under I love with all my love: for they go over.

## 7

To be true: few can do that! And whoever can, does not yet want to! But the good can do it least of all.

Oh, these good people! Good people never tell the truth; for the spirit, being good in this way is a sickness.

They give way, these good people, they give in, their heart repeats what it hears, their ground obeys: but whoever obeys does not hear himself!

Everything the good call evil must come together for one truth to be born: O my brothers, are you evil enough for this truth?

The daring venture, the long mistrust, the cruel No, the disgust, the cutting into the living: how seldom does that come together! But from such seed truth is begotten!

Beside the bad conscience all knowledge has grown until now! Break, break for me, you seekers of knowledge, the old tablets!

## 8

When the water has planks over it, when footbridges and railings span the river: truly, no one is believed then who says, “Everything is in flux.”

Rather, even the blockheads contradict him. “What?” say the blockheads, “everything is in flux? But the planks and the railings are above the river!

Above the river everything is firm, all the values of things, the bridges, concepts, all ‘good’ and ‘evil’: all of that is firm!”

But when the hard winter comes, the tamer of rivers, then even the wittiest learn mistrust; and truly, then it is not only the blockheads who say: “Should not everything stand still?”

“At bottom everything stands still”: that is a proper winter teaching, a good thing for a barren time, a good comfort for those who hibernate and sit by the stove.

“At bottom everything stands still”: but against this the thaw wind preaches!

The thaw wind, a bull that is no ploughing bull: a raging bull, a destroyer, that breaks ice with angry horns! But ice breaks footbridges!

O my brothers, is not everything in flux now? Have not all railings and footbridges fallen into the water? Who would still hold on to “good” and “evil”?

“Woe to us! Hail to us! The thaw wind is blowing!” Preach this for me, O my brothers, through all the streets!

## 9

There is an old illusion that is called good and evil. Until now the wheel of this illusion has turned around soothsayers and astrologers.

Once people believed in soothsayers and astrologers: and so they believed, “Everything is fate: you shall, because you must!”

Then again they mistrusted all soothsayers and astrologers: and so they believed, “Everything is freedom: you can, because you will!”

O my brothers, about stars and the future there has so far only been illusion, not knowledge: and so about good and evil there has so far only been illusion, not knowledge!

## 10

“You shall not rob! You shall not kill!” Such words were once called holy; before them people bent their knees and heads and took off their shoes.

But I ask you: where were there ever better robbers and killers in the world than such holy words?

Is there not in all life itself robbing and killing? And by calling such words holy, was not truth itself killed?

Or was it a preaching of death that called holy what contradicted and advised against all life? O my brothers, break, break for me the old tablets!

## 11

This is my pity for everything past, that I see it is abandoned:

abandoned to the favour, the spirit, the madness of every generation that comes and reinterprets everything that was as its bridge!

A great despot could come, a cunning monster, who with his favour and disfavour would force and strain everything past until it became for him a bridge and an omen and a herald and a cock-crow.

But this is the other danger and my other pity: whoever is of the mob, his memory goes back to his grandfather; but with the grandfather time stops.

So everything past is abandoned: for it could one day happen that the mob became master and drowned all time in shallow waters.

That is why, O my brothers, a new nobility is needed, which is the adversary of all mob and all despotism, and writes anew on new tablets the word “noble”.

For many noble ones are needed, and many kinds of noble ones, for there to be nobility! Or, as I once said in a parable: “Just this is godliness, that there are gods but no God!”

## 12

O my brothers, I consecrate you and direct you to a new nobility: you shall become begetters and breeders and sowers of the future;

truly, not to a nobility that you could buy like shopkeepers and with shopkeepers’ gold: for everything that has its price is of little worth.

Let it be your honour from now on, not where you come from, but where you are going! Your will and your foot, which wants to go beyond yourselves: let that be your new honour!

Truly not that you have served a prince (what do princes matter now!), or became a bulwark for what stands, so that it might stand more firmly!

Not that your family became courtly at courts, and you learned, bright like a flamingo, to stand for long hours in shallow ponds.

For being able to stand is a merit among courtiers; and all courtiers believe that being allowed to sit belongs to blessedness after death!

Nor that a spirit they call holy led your ancestors into promised lands, which I do not praise: for where the worst of all trees grew, the cross, there is nothing to praise in that land!

and truly, wherever this “holy spirit” led its knights, goats and geese and crossheads and cranks always ran in front on such expeditions!

O my brothers, your nobility should not look back, but out! You shall be exiles from all fatherlands and forefathers’ lands!

You shall love your children’s land: let this love be your new nobility, the undiscovered land, in the farthest sea! For it I bid your sails search and search!

In your children you shall make up for being the children of your fathers: so you shall redeem everything past! This new tablet I set up over you!

## 13

“Why live? All is vanity! Living: that is threshing straw; living: that is burning oneself and still not getting warm.”

Such ancient chatter still counts as “wisdom”; and because it is old and smells musty, it is honoured all the more. Even mould ennobles.

Children might talk like that: they fear the fire because it burned them! There is much childishness in the old books of wisdom.

And whoever is always “threshing straw”, how should he be allowed to slander threshing! Such a fool ought to have his mouth tied up!

Such people sit down at table and bring nothing with them, not even a good appetite: and now they slander, “All is vanity!”

But to eat and drink well, O my brothers, is truly no vain art! Break, break for me the tablets of the never-joyful!

## 14

“To the pure all things are pure”: so the people say. But I tell you: to swine all things become swine!

That is why the fanatics and the head-hangers, whose hearts hang down too, preach: “The world itself is a filthy monster.”

For these all have unclean spirits; but especially those who have no rest or peace unless they see the world from behind: the believers in a world behind!

To them I say to their faces, though it does not sound nice: the world is like man in this, that it has a behind: so much is true!

There is much filth in the world: so much is true! But that does not make the world itself a filthy monster!

There is wisdom in the fact that much in the world smells bad: disgust itself creates wings and powers that sense the presence of springs!

Even in the best there is something to be disgusted at; and even the best is something that must be overcome!

O my brothers, there is much wisdom in the fact that there is much filth in the world!

## 15

I heard pious believers in a world behind say such sayings to their consciences; and truly, without malice or falsehood, though there is nothing in the world more false, nor more malicious.

“Let the world be the world! Do not raise even one finger against it!”

“Let whoever wants to strangle and stab and cut and scrape people: do not raise even one finger against it! That way they will learn to renounce the world.”

“And your own reason: you yourself should choke and strangle it; for it is a reason of this world; that way you yourself will learn to renounce the world.”

Break, break for me, O my brothers, these old tablets of the pious! Break up the sayings of the slanderers of the world!
