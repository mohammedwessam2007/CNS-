## On Reading and Writing

Of all that is written, I love only what a man writes with his blood. Write with blood, and you will learn that blood is spirit.

It is not easy to understand someone else’s blood: I hate the idle who read.

Whoever knows the reader does nothing more for the reader. Another century of readers, and the spirit itself will stink.

That everyone may learn to read spoils, in the long run, not only writing but thinking too.

Once spirit was God; then it became man; and now it is even becoming the mob.

Whoever writes in blood and in sayings does not want to be read, but learned by heart.

In the mountains the shortest way is from peak to peak; but for that you need long legs. Sayings should be peaks, and those they are spoken to should be tall and great.

The air thin and pure, danger near, and the spirit full of a cheerful malice: these go well together.

I want goblins around me, for I am brave. Courage that scares away ghosts makes goblins for itself: courage wants to laugh.

I no longer feel with you. This cloud I see beneath me, this blackness and heaviness I laugh at: that is exactly your thundercloud.

You look upward when you long to be lifted up. And I look down, because I am lifted up.

Who among you can laugh and be lifted up at the same time?

Whoever climbs the highest mountains laughs at all tragedies, in plays and in life.

Brave, carefree, mocking, violent: so wisdom wants us. She is a woman, and only ever loves a warrior.

You tell me, “Life is hard to bear.” But why else would you have your pride in the morning and your resignation in the evening?

Life is hard to bear; but do not act so tender! We are all of us fine, sturdy, load-bearing donkeys, male and female.

What do we have in common with the rosebud, which trembles because a drop of dew lies on its body?

It is true: we love life, not because we are used to living, but because we are used to loving.

There is always some madness in love. But there is always some reason in madness too.

And to me as well, who am fond of life, it seems that butterflies and soap bubbles, and whatever is like them among people, know most about happiness.

To see these light, foolish, delicate, nimble little souls flutter about: that moves Zarathustra to tears and songs.

I would believe only in a god who knew how to dance.

And when I saw my devil, I found him serious, thorough, deep, solemn: he was the spirit of gravity; through him all things fall.

One kills not with anger but with laughter. Come, let us kill the spirit of gravity!

I have learned to walk; since then I let myself run. I have learned to fly; since then I do not want to be pushed before I move.

Now I am light, now I fly, now I see myself beneath me, now a god dances through me.

Thus spoke Zarathustra.

## On the Tree on the Mountainside

Zarathustra’s eye had seen that a young man was avoiding him. And one evening, as he walked alone through the hills around the town called “The Motley Cow”, he found this young man as he went, sitting leaning against a tree and gazing wearily into the valley. Zarathustra took hold of the tree beside which the young man sat, and spoke thus:

“If I wanted to shake this tree with my hands, I could not do it.

But the wind, which we do not see, torments it and bends it wherever it likes. We are bent and tormented worst by invisible hands.”

At this the young man got up in dismay and said: “I hear Zarathustra, and I was just thinking of him.” Zarathustra replied:

“Why does that frighten you? But it is the same with man as with the tree.

The more he wants to rise into the heights and the light, the more strongly his roots strive earthward, downward, into the dark, the deep: into evil.”

“Yes, into evil!” cried the young man. “How is it possible that you have discovered my soul?”

Zarathustra smiled and said: “Many a soul will never be discovered, unless it is first invented.”

“Yes, into evil!” cried the young man again. “You spoke the truth, Zarathustra. I no longer trust myself since I have wanted to climb, and no one trusts me any more. How does this happen?

I change too quickly: my today refutes my yesterday. I often skip the steps when I climb, and no step forgives me for that.

When I am up there, I always find myself alone. No one speaks to me; the frost of loneliness makes me shiver. What do I want in the heights?

My contempt and my longing grow together; the higher I climb, the more I despise the one who climbs. What does he want in the heights?

How ashamed I am of my climbing and stumbling! How I mock my violent panting! How I hate the one who flies! How tired I am in the heights!”

Here the young man fell silent. And Zarathustra looked at the tree beside which they stood, and spoke thus:

“This tree stands alone here on the mountain; it has grown high above man and beast.

And if it wanted to speak, it would have no one who understood it: so high has it grown.

Now it waits and waits. What is it waiting for? It lives too near the seat of the clouds: perhaps it is waiting for the first lightning?”

When Zarathustra had said this, the young man cried out with violent gestures: “Yes, Zarathustra, you speak the truth. I longed for my going-under when I wanted to climb, and you are the lightning I was waiting for! Look, what am I still, since you appeared to us? It is envy of you that has destroyed me!” So spoke the young man, and wept bitterly. But Zarathustra put his arm round him and led him away with him.

And when they had walked together for a while, Zarathustra began to speak thus:

“It breaks my heart. Better than your words say it, your eye tells me all your danger.

You are not yet free; you are still searching for freedom. Your searching has made you sleepless and over-awake.

You want to reach the free heights; your soul thirsts for the stars. But your bad instincts thirst for freedom too.

Your wild dogs want to be free; they bark with joy in their cellar when your spirit tries to open all the prisons.

To me you are still a prisoner who imagines freedom. Ah, the soul of such prisoners becomes clever, but also cunning and bad.

The one who has freed his spirit must still purify himself. Much prison and mould is still left in him: his eye must still become pure.

Yes, I know your danger. But by my love and hope I beg you: do not throw away your love and hope!

You still feel noble, and the others still feel you are noble, those who resent you and give you dirty looks. Know that a noble person stands in everyone’s way.

A noble person stands in the way of the good too; and even when they call him good, they mean by it to put him aside.

The noble person wants to create something new, and a new virtue. The good want the old, and that the old should be preserved.

But the danger for the noble person is not that he will become one of the good, but a scoffer, a mocker, a destroyer.

Ah, I have known noble people who lost their highest hope. And then they slandered all high hopes.

Then they lived shamelessly in brief pleasures, and hardly cast their aims beyond the day.

‘Spirit is also lust,’ they said. Then the wings of their spirit broke; now it crawls about and dirties whatever it gnaws.

Once they thought of becoming heroes; now they are pleasure-seekers. To them the hero is a grief and a horror.

But by my love and hope I beg you: do not throw away the hero in your soul! Keep holy your highest hope!”

Thus spoke Zarathustra.

## On the Preachers of Death

There are preachers of death; and the earth is full of those to whom turning away from life must be preached.

The earth is full of the superfluous; life is spoiled by the many-too-many. Let them be lured out of this life with “eternal life”!

“The yellow ones” or “the black ones”, that is what the preachers of death are called. But I want to show them to you in other colours too.

There are the terrible ones, who carry the beast of prey around inside themselves and have no choice except lusts or tearing themselves apart. And even their lusts are a tearing of themselves apart.

They have not even become human beings yet, these terrible ones. Let them preach turning away from life, and go away themselves!

There are the consumptives of the soul: scarcely are they born before they begin to die, and long for teachings of weariness and renunciation.

They would like to be dead, and we should approve of their wish! Let us beware of waking these dead and damaging these living coffins!

They meet a sick man, or an old man, or a corpse, and at once they say, “Life is refuted!”

But only they are refuted, and their eye, which sees only this one face of existence.

Wrapped in thick gloom, and eager for the little accidents that bring death: so they wait, and clench their teeth.

Or else they reach for sweets and mock their own childishness as they do it: they cling to their straw of life, and mock that they still cling to a straw.

Their wisdom runs: “A fool, who stays alive; but such fools we are! And that is the most foolish thing about life!”

“Life is only suffering,” say others, and they do not lie. Then see to it that you stop! See to it that the life stops which is only suffering!

And let the teaching of your virtue be: “You shall kill yourself! You shall steal yourself away!”

“Lust is sin,” say some who preach death; “let us go aside and beget no children!”

“Giving birth is hard,” say others; “why give birth any more? One gives birth only to the unhappy!” And they too are preachers of death.

“Pity is needed,” say a third kind. “Take what I have! Take what I am! The less life binds me!”

If they were pitying through and through, they would spoil life for their neighbours. To be wicked: that would be their true goodness.

But they want to get free of life. What do they care if they bind others still tighter with their chains and gifts!

And you too, for whom life is furious work and unrest: are you not very tired of life? Are you not very ripe for the preaching of death?

All of you who love furious work, and the fast, the new, the strange: you bear yourselves badly; your diligence is flight, and the will to forget yourselves.

If you believed more in life, you would throw yourselves less into the moment. But you have not enough content in you for waiting, nor even for laziness!

Everywhere sounds the voice of those who preach death; and the earth is full of those to whom death must be preached.

Or “eternal life”: it is all the same to me, as long as they go away quickly!

Thus spoke Zarathustra.

## On War and Warriors

We do not want to be spared by our best enemies, nor by those we love from the bottom of our hearts. So let me tell you the truth!

My brothers in war! I love you from the bottom of my heart; I am and was one of you. And I am also your best enemy. So let me tell you the truth!

I know the hatred and envy of your hearts. You are not great enough not to know hatred and envy. Then be great enough not to be ashamed of them!

And if you cannot be saints of knowledge, then at least be its warriors. They are the companions and forerunners of such holiness.

I see many soldiers: I wish I saw many warriors! What they wear is called a “uniform”: may what they hide in it not be uniform!

You should be such as are always looking for an enemy: for your enemy. And with some of you there is hatred at first sight.

You should seek your enemy, wage your war, and for your thoughts! And if your thought is defeated, your honesty should still shout triumph over it!

You should love peace as a means to new wars, and the short peace more than the long.

I do not advise you to work, but to fight. I do not advise you to make peace, but to win. Let your work be a fight, let your peace be a victory!

One can be silent and sit still only when one has a bow and arrows; otherwise one chatters and quarrels. Let your peace be a victory!

You say it is the good cause that hallows even war? I tell you: it is the good war that hallows every cause.

War and courage have done more great things than love of one’s neighbour. Not your pity but your bravery has saved the unfortunate until now.

“What is good?” you ask. To be brave is good. Let the little girls say: “Good is what is pretty and touching at the same time.”

They call you heartless; but your heart is genuine, and I love the shame of your warm-heartedness. You are ashamed of your flood, and others are ashamed of their ebb.

You are ugly? Well then, my brothers! Wrap the sublime around you, the cloak of the ugly!

And when your soul grows great, it grows arrogant, and in your sublimity there is malice. I know you.

In malice the arrogant man meets the weakling. But they misunderstand each other. I know you.

You may have only enemies to be hated, not enemies to be despised. You must be proud of your enemy: then your enemy’s successes are your successes too.

Rebellion: that is the nobility of the slave. Let your nobility be obedience! Let your very commanding be an obeying!

To a good warrior “you shall” sounds more pleasant than “I will”. And everything you love, you should first have commanded to you.

Let your love of life be love of your highest hope; and let your highest hope be the highest thought of life!

But your highest thought you should let me command you, and it runs: man is something that shall be overcome.

So live your life of obedience and of war! What does long life matter! What warrior wants to be spared!

I do not spare you, I love you from the bottom of my heart, my brothers in war!

Thus spoke Zarathustra.

## On the New Idol

Somewhere there are still peoples and herds, but not with us, my brothers: here there are states.

The state? What is that? Well then! Now open your ears to me, for now I shall tell you my word about the death of peoples.

State is the name of the coldest of all cold monsters. Coldly it lies, too; and this lie crawls out of its mouth: “I, the state, am the people.”

It is a lie! It was creators who created peoples and hung a faith and a love over them: so they served life.

It is destroyers who set traps for the many and call them the state: they hang a sword and a hundred appetites over them.

Where there is still a people, it does not understand the state, and hates it as the evil eye and a sin against customs and rights.

This sign I give you: every people speaks its own tongue of good and evil, which its neighbour does not understand. It invented its own language in customs and rights.

But the state lies in all the tongues of good and evil; and whatever it says, it lies; and whatever it has, it has stolen.

Everything about it is false; it bites with stolen teeth, the biter. Even its insides are false.

Confusion of the tongues of good and evil: this sign I give you as the sign of the state. Truly, this sign points to the will to death! Truly, it beckons to the preachers of death!

Far too many are born: for the superfluous the state was invented!

Just look how it lures them to itself, the many-too-many! How it swallows them and chews them and chews them again!

“On earth there is nothing greater than I: I am the ordering finger of God,” so the monster roars. And it is not only the long-eared and the short-sighted who sink to their knees!

Ah, even in you, you great souls, it whispers its dark lies! Ah, it guesses out the rich hearts that like to squander themselves!

Yes, it guesses you out too, you conquerors of the old god! You grew tired in the fight, and now your tiredness serves the new idol!

It would like to set up heroes and honourable men around itself, the new idol! It likes to bask in the sunshine of good consciences, the cold monster!

It will give you everything, if you worship it, the new idol: so it buys the shine of your virtue and the look of your proud eyes.

It wants to use you as bait for the many-too-many! Yes, a hellish trick was invented there, a horse of death, jingling in the finery of divine honours!

Yes, a dying for many was invented there, which praises itself as life: truly, a heartfelt service to all preachers of death!

I call it the state where all drink poison, good and bad alike; the state, where all lose themselves, good and bad alike; the state, where the slow suicide of all is called “life”.

Just look at these superfluous ones! They steal the works of the inventors and the treasures of the wise; they call their theft “education”, and everything turns into sickness and trouble for them!

Just look at these superfluous ones! They are always sick; they vomit their bile and call it a newspaper. They devour one another and cannot even digest themselves.

Just look at these superfluous ones! They acquire riches and become poorer by them. They want power, and first of all the crowbar of power, much money; these incapable ones!

See them climb, these nimble apes! They climb over one another and so drag one another into the mud and the depths.

They all want to get to the throne: that is their madness, as if happiness sat on the throne! Often mud sits on the throne, and often the throne on the mud.

Madmen they all are to me, and climbing apes, and overheated. Their idol smells bad to me, the cold monster; they all smell bad to me together, these idolaters.

My brothers, do you want to suffocate in the fumes of their mouths and appetites? Rather break the windows and jump out into the open!

Get away from the bad smell! Get away from the idolatry of the superfluous!

Get away from the bad smell! Get away from the steam of these human sacrifices!

The earth is still free for great souls. Many places are still empty for the solitary and for pairs, where the smell of quiet seas blows.

A free life is still open to great souls. Truly, whoever owns little is owned that much less: blessed be a little poverty!

There, where the state ends, there first begins the human being who is not superfluous: there begins the song of the necessary, the single and irreplaceable melody.

There, where the state ends: look there, my brothers! Do you not see it, the rainbow and the bridges of the Overman?

Thus spoke Zarathustra.

## On the Flies of the Market Place

Flee, my friend, into your solitude! I see you deafened by the noise of the great men and stung by the stings of the small.

Forest and rock know how to be silent with you, with dignity. Be like the tree you love again, the broad-branched one: silent and listening, it leans over the sea.

Where solitude ends, the market place begins; and where the market place begins, there begins also the noise of the great actors and the buzzing of the poisonous flies.

In the world even the best things are worth nothing without someone who first puts them on stage: the people call these showmen great men.

The people understand little of what is great, that is, of what creates. But they have a sense for all showmen and actors of great things.

The world turns around the inventors of new values: invisibly it turns. But around the actors turn the people and fame: such is the way of the world.

The actor has spirit, but little conscience of the spirit. He always believes in whatever makes people believe most strongly: believe in him!

Tomorrow he has a new faith, and the day after tomorrow a newer one. He has quick senses, like the people, and changeable moods.

To overthrow: that, to him, means to prove. To drive mad: that, to him, means to convince. And blood counts to him as the best of all reasons.

A truth that slips only into fine ears he calls a lie and nothing. Truly, he believes only in gods that make a great noise in the world!

The market place is full of solemn buffoons, and the people boast of their great men! These are their masters of the hour.

But the hour presses them; so they press you. And from you too they want a Yes or a No. Alas, do you want to set your chair between For and Against?

Do not be jealous of these unconditional and pressing people, you lover of truth! Never yet has truth hung on the arm of the unconditional.

Because of these sudden people, go back into your security: only in the market place is one attacked with “Yes?” or “No?”

Slow is the experience of all deep wells: they must wait long before they know what has fallen into their depths.

Everything great happens away from the market place and from fame: the inventors of new values have always lived away from the market place and from fame.

Flee, my friend, into your solitude: I see you stung by poisonous flies. Flee to where a rough, strong air blows!

Flee into your solitude! You lived too near the small and the pitiful. Flee from their invisible revenge! Towards you they are nothing but revenge.

Raise your arm against them no more! They are countless, and it is not your lot to be a fly-swatter.

Countless are these small and pitiful ones; and raindrops and weeds have already brought many a proud building to ruin.

You are not a stone, but already many drops have made you hollow. You will yet break and burst from many drops.

I see you wearied by poisonous flies, I see you scratched bloody in a hundred places; and your pride will not even be angry.

They want blood from you in all innocence; their bloodless souls crave blood, and so they sting in all innocence.

But you, deep one, you suffer too deeply even from small wounds; and before you have healed, the same poisonous worm has crawled over your hand.

You are too proud to kill these greedy little things. But beware that it does not become your fate to bear all their poisonous injustice!

They buzz around you with their praise too: their praise is pushiness. They want to be near your skin and your blood.

They flatter you as they would a god or a devil; they whine before you as before a god or a devil. What does it matter! They are flatterers and whiners, and nothing more.

They often show themselves to you as lovable, too. But that has always been the cleverness of cowards. Yes, cowards are clever!

They think a great deal about you with their narrow souls; you are always worrying to them! Whatever is thought about a great deal becomes worrying.

They punish you for all your virtues. From the bottom of their hearts they forgive you only your mistakes.

Because you are gentle and just in mind, you say: “They are innocent of their small existence.” But their narrow souls think: “All great existence is guilt.”

Even when you are gentle with them, they still feel despised by you; and they repay your kindness with hidden unkindness.

Your wordless pride always goes against their taste; they rejoice when for once you are modest enough to be vain.

What we recognise in a person, we also set alight in him. So beware of the small!

In your presence they feel small, and their lowness smoulders and glows against you in invisible revenge.

Did you not notice how often they fell silent when you came up to them, and how their strength left them like smoke from a dying fire?

Yes, my friend, you are the bad conscience of your neighbours, for they are unworthy of you. So they hate you, and would like to suck your blood.

Your neighbours will always be poisonous flies; what is great in you, that itself must make them more poisonous and ever more like flies.

Flee, my friend, into your solitude, and to where a rough, strong air blows. It is not your lot to be a fly-swatter.

Thus spoke Zarathustra.

## On Chastity

I love the forest. It is bad to live in cities: there are too many there who are in heat.

Is it not better to fall into the hands of a murderer than into the dreams of a woman in heat?

And just look at these men: their eye says it, they know nothing better on earth than to lie with a woman.

There is mud at the bottom of their souls; and woe if their mud has spirit too!

If only you were at least perfect as animals! But innocence belongs to the animal.

Do I advise you to kill your senses? I advise you to the innocence of the senses.

Do I advise you to chastity? Chastity is a virtue in some, but in many it is almost a vice.

These people hold back, it is true; but the bitch Sensuality looks out with envy from everything they do.

Even into the heights of their virtue, and right into their cold spirit, this beast and its unrest follow them.

And how nicely the bitch Sensuality knows how to beg for a piece of spirit when a piece of meat is denied her!

You love tragedies, and everything that breaks the heart? But I am suspicious of your bitch.

Your eyes are too cruel for me, and you look lustfully at those who suffer. Has not your lust merely disguised itself and called itself pity?

And this parable too I give you: not a few who wanted to drive out their devil went into the swine themselves.

To whom chastity is hard, it should be advised against, so that it does not become the road to hell: that is, to the mud and heat of the soul.

Do I speak of dirty things? That is not the worst to me.

Not when truth is dirty, but when it is shallow, is the one who seeks knowledge reluctant to step into its water.

Truly, there are those who are chaste through and through: they are gentler of heart, they laugh more gladly and more richly than you.

They laugh at chastity too, and ask: “What is chastity?

Is chastity not foolishness? But this foolishness came to us, and not we to it.

We offered this guest shelter and heart: now it lives with us; let it stay as long as it likes!”

Thus spoke Zarathustra.
