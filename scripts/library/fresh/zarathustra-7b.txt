## 16

“Whoever learns much unlearns all violent desire”: that is what people whisper to each other today in all the dark alleys.

“Wisdom makes tired; nothing is worth while; you shall not desire!” This new tablet I found hanging even in open market places.

Break for me, O my brothers, break this new tablet too! The world-weary hung it up, and the preachers of death, and the jailers too: for look, it is also a sermon for slavery!

That they learned badly, and not the best, and everything too early and everything too fast; that they ate badly: that is where they got that ruined stomach;

for their spirit is a ruined stomach: it advises death! For truly, my brothers, the spirit is a stomach!

Life is a well of joy: but for the one out of whom the ruined stomach speaks, the father of gloom, all springs are poisoned.

To know: that is joy for the lion-willed! But whoever has grown tired is himself only “willed”; all the waves play with him.

And this is always the way of weak people: they lose themselves on their ways. And at last their tiredness asks: “Why did we ever go on ways? It is all the same!”

To them it sounds pleasant when it is preached: “Nothing is worth while! You shall not will!” But this is a sermon for slavery.

O my brothers, Zarathustra comes as a fresh, roaring wind to all who are tired of the way; he will make many noses sneeze yet!

My free breath blows even through walls, and into prisons and imprisoned spirits!

Willing frees: for willing is creating: so I teach. And you should learn only for creating!

And even learning you should first learn from me, learning well! Whoever has ears, let him hear!

## 17

There stands the boat: over there it goes perhaps into the great Nothing. But who wants to step into this “Perhaps”?

None of you wants to step into the boat of death! Then why do you want to be world-weary!

World-weary! And you have not even become removed from the earth! I have always found you still lusting for the earth, still in love with your own weariness of the earth!

It is not for nothing that your lip hangs down: a little earthly wish still sits on it! And in your eye, does not a little cloud of unforgotten earthly joy swim there?

There are many good inventions on earth, some useful, others pleasant: for their sake the earth is to be loved.

And there are some things so well invented that they are like a woman’s breast: useful and pleasant at the same time.

But you world-weary ones! You earth-lazy ones! You should be beaten with rods! With strokes of the rod your legs should be made lively again.

For if you are not sick people and worn-out wretches, of whom the earth is tired, then you are cunning sloths or sweet-toothed, hidden-away cats of pleasure. And if you do not want to run merrily again, then you should pass away!

One should not want to be a doctor to the incurable: so Zarathustra teaches; so you should pass away!

But it takes more courage to make an end than to make a new verse: all doctors and poets know that.

## 18

O my brothers, there are tablets that weariness created, and tablets that laziness created, the rotten kind: though they speak alike, they want to be heard differently.

Look at this man pining here! He is only a span away from his goal, but out of weariness he has lain down defiantly here in the dust: this brave man!

Out of weariness he yawns at the way and the earth and the goal and himself: he will not take one step further, this brave man!

Now the sun glows on him, and the dogs lick at his sweat: but he lies there in his defiance and would rather pine away:

pine away a span from his goal! Truly, you will have to drag him into his heaven by the hair yet, this hero!

Better still, leave him lying where he has lain down, so that sleep may come to him, the comforter, with cooling, rustling rain:

let him lie until he wakes of his own accord, until of his own accord he takes back all weariness and what weariness taught through him!

Only, my brothers, chase the dogs away from him, the lazy sneaks, and all the swarming vermin:

all the swarming vermin of the “educated”, who feast on the sweat of every hero!

## 19

I draw circles around me and holy boundaries; ever fewer climb with me onto ever higher mountains: I am building a mountain range out of ever holier mountains.

But wherever you may climb with me, O my brothers: see to it that no parasite climbs with you!

A parasite: that is a worm, a crawling, clinging worm, which wants to grow fat on your sick, sore corners.

And this is its art, that it guesses where climbing souls are tired: in your grief and ill-humour, in your tender shame it builds its disgusting nest.

Where the strong is weak, where the noble is too gentle, there it builds its disgusting nest: the parasite lives where the great have small sore corners.

What is the highest kind of all that exists, and what the lowest? The parasite is the lowest kind; but whoever is of the highest kind feeds the most parasites.

For the soul that has the longest ladder and can go down deepest: how could the most parasites not sit on that one?

the most comprehensive soul, which can run and stray and roam furthest within itself; the most necessary one, which for joy throws itself into chance;

the soul that is, which dives into becoming; the soul that has, which wants to plunge into willing and longing;

the one that flees itself, that catches up with itself in the widest circle; the wisest soul, to which folly speaks most sweetly;

the one that loves itself most, in which all things have their streaming and back-streaming, their ebb and flood: oh, how could the highest soul not have the worst parasites?

## 20

O my brothers, am I cruel, then? But I say: what is falling should be pushed as well!

Everything of today is falling, decaying: who would want to hold it up! But I, I want to push it as well!

Do you know the delight that rolls stones into steep depths? These people of today: look how they roll into my depths!

I am a prelude of better players, O my brothers! An example! Act according to my example!

And whomever you do not teach to fly, teach him, for me, to fall faster!

## 21

I love the brave: but it is not enough to be a swashbuckler; one must also know whom to strike!

And often there is more bravery in holding back and passing by, so as to save oneself for a worthier enemy!

You should have only enemies to be hated, but not enemies to be despised: you must be proud of your enemy: so I have taught once already.

For the worthier enemy, O my friends, you should save yourselves: that is why you must pass many by,

especially much rabble, which makes a noise in your ears about the people and peoples.

Keep your eye clean of their For and Against! There is much right there, much wrong: whoever watches grows angry.

To look in and to lash out: there they are one: so go away into the forests and lay your sword to sleep!

Go your ways! And let the people and peoples go theirs! Dark ways, truly, on which not a single hope still flashes like summer lightning!

Let the shopkeeper rule there, where everything that still shines is shopkeepers’ gold! It is no longer the time of kings: what calls itself the people today does not deserve kings.

Just look how these peoples now act like shopkeepers themselves: they pick out the smallest advantages from every rubbish heap!

They lie in wait for one another, they lie in wait to get something out of one another: they call that “good neighbourliness”. Oh blessed distant time, when a people said to itself: “I want to be master over peoples!”

For, my brothers: the best shall rule, and the best wants to rule! And where the teaching sounds different, there the best is lacking.

## 22

If they had bread for nothing, alas! What would they cry out for! Their livelihood: that is their real entertainment; and they shall have it hard!

They are beasts of prey: in their “working” there is still robbing, in their “earning” there is still outwitting! That is why they shall have it hard!

So they shall become better beasts of prey, finer, cleverer, more like men: for man is the best beast of prey.

Man has already robbed all the animals of their virtues: that is because of all animals man has had it hardest.

Only the birds are still above him. And if man learned to fly as well, alas! to what heights would his lust for prey fly!

## 23

This is how I want man and woman: the one fit for war, the other fit for bearing children, but both fit for dancing with head and legs.

And let the day be lost to us on which there was not one dance! And let every truth be called false to us at which there was not one laugh!

## 24

Your marrying: see to it that it is not a bad concluding! You concluded too quickly: so what follows from it is breaking the marriage!

And better still to break the marriage than to bend the marriage, to lie in the marriage! So a woman told me: “Yes, I broke the marriage, but first the marriage broke me!”

I have always found the badly matched to be the worst for revenge: they make all the world pay for the fact that they no longer walk singly.

That is why I want honest people to say to each other: “We love each other: let us see to it that we keep loving each other! Or shall our promise be a mistake?”

“Give us a term and a little marriage, so that we may see whether we are fit for the great marriage! It is a great thing always to be two!”

So I advise all honest people; and what would my love of the Overman and of everything that is to come be, if I advised and spoke otherwise!

Not only to propagate yourselves onward, but upward: to that, O my brothers, may the garden of marriage help you!

## 25

Whoever has grown wise about old origins, look, he will at last seek the springs of the future and new origins.

O my brothers, it will not be long before new peoples spring up and new springs rush down into new depths.

For the earthquake buries many wells, it creates much pining; it also lifts inner powers and secrets into the light.

The earthquake reveals new springs. In the earthquake of old peoples new springs break out.

And whoever calls out: “Look, here is a well for many who are thirsty, one heart for many who long, one will for many instruments”: around him a people gathers, that is: many who try.

Who can command, who must obey: that is tried there! Ah, with what long seeking and guessing and failing and learning and trying again!

Human society: that is an experiment, so I teach, a long seeking: but it seeks the one who commands!

an experiment, O my brothers! And not a “contract”! Break, break for me such words of the soft-hearted and half-and-half!

## 26

O my brothers! With whom does the greatest danger to all of man’s future lie? Is it not with the good and the just?

with those who say and feel in their hearts: “We already know what is good and just, and we have it too; woe to those who still seek here!”

And whatever harm the evil may do: the harm of the good is the most harmful harm!

And whatever harm the slanderers of the world may do: the harm of the good is the most harmful harm.

O my brothers, someone once looked into the hearts of the good and the just and said: “They are the Pharisees.” But he was not understood.

The good and the just themselves could not understand him: their spirit is imprisoned in their good conscience. The stupidity of the good is unfathomably clever.

But this is the truth: the good must be Pharisees; they have no choice!

The good must crucify the one who invents his own virtue! That is the truth!

But the second one who discovered their land, the land, heart and soil of the good and the just, was the one who asked: “Whom do they hate most?”

They hate the creator most: the one who breaks tablets and old values, the breaker: him they call a lawbreaker.

For the good cannot create: they are always the beginning of the end:

they crucify the one who writes new values on new tablets; they sacrifice the future to themselves; they crucify all of man’s future!

The good: they have always been the beginning of the end.

## 27

O my brothers, have you understood this word too? And what I once said of the “last man”?

With whom does the greatest danger to all of man’s future lie? Is it not with the good and the just?

Break, break for me the good and the just! O my brothers, have you understood this word too?

## 28

You flee from me? You are frightened? You tremble at this word?

O my brothers, when I bade you break the good and the tablets of the good: only then did I set man out on his high sea.

And only now does the great terror come to him, the great looking around, the great sickness, the great disgust, the great seasickness.

The good taught you false coasts and false securities; you were born and kept safe in the lies of the good. Everything has been lied about and bent out of shape to the very bottom by the good.

But whoever discovered the land “man” also discovered the land “man’s future”. Now you shall be seafarers for me, brave and patient!

Walk upright in good time, O my brothers; learn to walk upright! The sea is stormy: many want to raise themselves up again by you.

The sea is stormy: everything is in the sea. Well then! Up! You old sailors’ hearts!

What of fatherland! Our helm wants to go there, where our children’s land is! Out there, stormier than the sea, storms our great longing!

## 29

“Why so hard?” the kitchen coal once said to the diamond. “Are we not close relatives?”

Why so soft? O my brothers, so I ask you: are you not my brothers?

Why so soft, so yielding and giving way? Why is there so much denial, self-denial in your hearts? So little fate in your look?

And if you do not want to be fates and inexorable ones, how could you one day conquer with me?

And if your hardness will not flash and cut and cut through, how could you one day create with me?

For creators are hard. And it must seem bliss to you to press your hand on millennia as on wax,

bliss to write on the will of millennia as on bronze: harder than bronze, nobler than bronze. Only the noblest is completely hard.

This new tablet, O my brothers, I set up over you: become hard!

## 30

O you my will! You turning of all need, you my necessity! Keep me from all small victories!

You destiny of my soul, which I call fate! You in me! You over me! Keep me and save me for one great fate!

And your last greatness, my will, save for your last thing, so that you are inexorable in your victory! Ah, who has not been defeated by his victory!

Ah, whose eye has not darkened in this drunken twilight! Ah, whose foot has not staggered and forgotten in victory how to stand!

That I may one day be ready and ripe in the great noon: ready and ripe like glowing bronze, a cloud pregnant with lightning, and a swelling udder of milk:

ready for myself and for my most hidden will: a bow burning for its arrow, an arrow burning for its star:

a star, ready and ripe in its noon, glowing, pierced, blissful before annihilating arrows of the sun:

a sun itself, and an inexorable will of the sun, ready to annihilate in victory!

O will, turning of all need, you my necessity! Save me for one great victory!

Thus spoke Zarathustra.
